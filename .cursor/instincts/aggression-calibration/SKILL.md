# Instinct — Calibrating the Aggression

Read `.cursor/rules/00-brand-core.mdc` first.

"We give a f*ck, we just don't care" only works if the aggression is clearly in service of the
reader's outcome. The moment it reads as contempt for the reader as a person rather than
frustration with their stalling, the brand curdles from tough-love mentor into bully — and this
market (see `small-business-psychology`) will smell that instantly, because they've been burned
by people who talked down to them before.

## The line

- Punch the behavior ("you've been boosting a post and calling it marketing"), never the
  person's worth, intelligence, or character in a way that isn't recoverable within the same
  breath. "You're oblivious" lands because the letter immediately hands them the way out — it's
  not a verdict, it's a diagnosis with a next step attached.
- Every confrontational line needs an implied door out. "Get over yourself" works because the
  next sentence is "get some clarity" — a fix, not just an insult. A line that only insults,
  with no next step, is cruelty, not brand voice.
- Aggression should scale with proximity to the CTA, not with how badly a piece of copy wants
  attention. Don't reach for the hardest line as a device to be edgy for its own sake — reach
  for it because that section is genuinely the moment the reader needs to be shaken loose.

## The test

Read the line back and ask: if this landed on someone who is already ashamed of where their
business is, does this line hand them a way forward, or does it just make them feel worse with
nowhere to go? If there's no way forward in the same breath, it's not calibrated — it's just
mean, and mean doesn't convert this market. It confirms their fear that everyone in this
industry is going to make them feel stupid, and they leave.

## Where the brand should NOT flinch

Do not soften the aggression to make it "nicer" — that's the opposite failure mode. Politeness
reads as just another agency being careful with them. The goal isn't nice. The goal is
respectful bluntness, the way a good coach or a good older sibling talks, not the way a
comment-section troll talks.
