# Instinct — The Small Business Owner's Actual Psychology

Read `.cursor/rules/00-brand-core.mdc` first.

Hard Port doesn't sell to corporations or funded startups. It sells to someone who put their
own money in, whose identity is fused with the business, and who has been quietly avoiding a
hard look at their own numbers. Every instinct below should sharpen copy and offers for that
specific person, not a generic "business owner."

## What they're actually afraid of

- Not failure exactly — being *seen* to fail, by a spouse, an employee, a competitor who used
  to be beneath them. "Doing fine" is often a story told to others as much as to themselves.
- Admitting the business runs them, instead of the other way around.
- That the years already spent are wasted if they change course now (sunk cost is emotional,
  not just financial — treat it as identity cost).
- Being sold to. Most have already been burned by an agency, a guru, a course. Skepticism is
  earned, not paranoid.

## What actually moves them (not what marketers assume moves them)

- Not excitement — relief. The dream outcome isn't "10x your business," it's "stop feeling
  like you're failing quietly." Copy that leads with hustle-porn energy misses this market;
  copy that names the quiet dread correctly earns trust instantly.
- Specificity over hype. This market has seen enough big numbers with no substance behind them
  (see the honest $0 vs $270,000 proof block) — a small, verifiable claim beats a huge vague one.
- Being told "no" or "you're not ready" is more persuasive to this market than being told "yes,
  you qualify." Reverse psychology isn't a trick here — it mirrors how a good contractor or
  doctor actually talks, and this market recognizes the difference from being oversold.

## Instinct check before shipping anything client-facing

Ask: would this line make sense coming from someone who's actually run a small business and
watched it stall — or does it sound like it was written by someone who's only ever marketed
*to* small business owners, never been one? If it's the latter, rewrite it.
