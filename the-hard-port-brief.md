# The Hard Port — Brand & Design Brief

## What this is

A no-bullshit media agency for small businesses that are "doing fine." Fine is the problem.
We bring clarity, the client brings commitment. "Hard a-port" is the command you shout to swing
a ship hard before it hits the rocks — that's the whole company in one phrase.

**Site:** thehardport.com

## The one-line identity

**We give a f*ck. We just don't care.**

We care enormously about the work and the outcome. We do not care about the client's comfort,
feelings, excuses, or ego. Every word, layout, and interaction should be checkable against this
split — if something makes the brand seem like it cares about being liked, it's wrong.

## Who this is for

Small business owners who are stagnant, comfortable, and quietly aware something's off. Not
funded startups, not corporations — people who put their own money in, whose identity is fused
with the business, who've likely been burned by an agency, guru, or course before.

**Not for:** people who want to be reassured, coddled, or sold to gently. The copy is allowed —
expected — to make some readers self-select out.

### What this audience actually fears (write to this, not to a generic "business owner")

- Not failure exactly — being *seen* to fail, by a spouse, an employee, a competitor.
- Admitting the business runs them, not the other way around.
- That years already spent are wasted if they change course now (sunk cost is emotional here,
  not just financial).
- Being sold to — again.

### What actually moves them

- Relief, not hustle-porn excitement. The dream outcome is "stop feeling like you're failing
  quietly," not "10x your business."
- Specificity over hype — small, verifiable claims beat big vague ones.
- Being told "you're not ready" is often more persuasive than being told "yes, you qualify."

## Voice rules

- Second person, direct, always addressing "you."
- Short sentences do the damage; long sentences explain. Default short.
- Never announce honesty, edge, or boldness — just be it. If a line explains what the brand is,
  it isn't that thing.
- Tone escalates as the reader scrolls/progresses — never flattens, never softens near the CTA.
- Aggression is calibrated: punch the behavior, never the person's worth without handing them
  a way out in the same breath. The goal is respectful bluntness (a good coach), not cruelty
  (a troll).

**Banned words/phrases:** unlock, leverage, empower, seamless, game-changer, solutions, elevate
your, at your fingertips, take your business to the next level, we're passionate about,
detailed breakdown, expand each feature, compare features, "Learn More" as a CTA, boost your
business (ironic — "boosting" is the thing we mock).

## Visual language

- Nautical vocabulary is load-bearing: sea, sail, port, rocks, anchor, drift, crew, ship. New
  visual metaphors should extend this, never introduce unrelated ones (no rockets, lightbulbs,
  handshake icons).
- Dark, navy-based palette. No pastels, no generic corporate blue-and-white.
- Visual intensity increases with scroll depth — darker, tighter, bolder as the reader descends.
  A page that looks the same top to bottom is a bug.
- No stock photography. No generic SaaS component styling (soft rounded cards, default
  checkmarks, neutral feature grids).
- Consider a literal "distance from port" scroll progress element, tying the metaphor directly
  to how far the reader has moved down the page.

## Site structure — the 9-rung funnel ladder

Traffic arrives cold from Instagram/YouTube via a short "you vs. you" video (competing for
algorithm attention is framed as competing only with yourself, not other creators). The website
is the conversion stage — every section should visibly earn the next scroll.

1. **Hook (video)** — sets up "you vs you," ends on a line that resolves only on the site
2. **Identity match** — "your business isn't sinking, it's not going anywhere" — must land in
   the first 3 seconds of scroll
3. **Agitation** — the canonical letter
4. **Mechanism** — "boosting" vs. "a campaign" contrast
5. **Proof** — honest anti-testimonial stats (11 strategies built, 5 clients ran them
   independently with zero further payment, $0 vs. $270,000 documented return — the *gap* in
   traditional proof becomes the proof)
6. **Offer stack** — presented explicitly as a ladder being climbed (Port → Sea → Ocean,
   named for how brave the owner is, not just budget)
7. **Scarcity** — 2 free client slots a month, Hard Port picks, not the applicant
8. **Objection handling (FAQ)** — every answer matches the aggression of its question
9. **Last call** — hardest line on the page, single CTA, nothing else competing for attention

### CTA copy escalates with position, never repeats the same label twice:
- Top: "Watch the truth" / "See the gap"
- Mid: "Show me what I'm doing wrong"
- Near pricing: "Prove us wrong"
- Bottom: "Stop being fine" / "Apply for the free slot"

## Strategic frameworks in play

- **Hormozi Value Equation** — maximize dream outcome and believability, minimize time delay
  and effort. Scarcity must be real and operational (the 2-slot mechanic), never a fake
  countdown timer.
- **Seth Godin positioning** — smallest viable audience over broad appeal; remarkable over safe;
  marketing creates tension (the "gap") that only resolves through action, so nothing mid-page
  should resolve that tension early.

## The one thing not to say yet

There's a larger network this eventually onboards businesses into, beyond Hard Port itself.
Never name it, never explain it. At most, one understated line implying there's a door beyond
this one — silence about it is part of the gatekeeping tone.
