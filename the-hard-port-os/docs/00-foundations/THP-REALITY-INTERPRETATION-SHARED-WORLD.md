# Reality, Interpretation, and the Shared World

> **Status:** `candidate`, `non_canonical`  
> **Nature:** Foundational philosophical position — not Canon, not empirical finding.  
> **Source:** Founder forms THP-PH-001 and THP-PH-001B ([`../../philosophy/PH-001.md`](../../philosophy/PH-001.md)). Institutional position below does **not** treat founder metaphysics as finalized fact.

## 1. Purpose

This position defines how The Hard Port currently distinguishes external conditions, personal experience, interpretation, shared reality, facts, truth, and expression.

It does not claim to resolve the ultimate metaphysical nature of reality or consciousness.

Operational definitions of conscious beings and systems remain in [`THP-FOUNDATIONAL-PHILOSOPHY.md`](./THP-FOUNDATIONAL-PHILOSOPHY.md). Epistemic rules remain in [`THP-EPISTEMOLOGY.md`](./THP-EPISTEMOLOGY.md).

## 2. External Existence

The Hard Port begins from the position that conditions, beings, events, and objects can exist independently of any one individual's interpretation.

An individual does not need to perceive something for it to exist.

However, something begins affecting that individual's experienced reality when it enters a relationship with them through perception, information, consequence, memory, imagination, or action.

THP therefore distinguishes **existence** from **experienced relevance**.

## 3. Personal Experience

Personal experience is the subjective response produced through an interaction between a person and something encountered, remembered, imagined, or anticipated.

Experience is shaped by factors including:

- physical condition;
- previous experiences;
- knowledge;
- attention;
- expectations;
- relationships;
- environment;
- values;
- and current emotional or cognitive conditions.

An experience can be real as an internal event without providing a completely accurate representation of external conditions.

**Distinction:** Subjective experience is real and consequential. It is not, by itself, proof of external accuracy.

## 4. Personal Interpretation

Personal interpretation is the meaning or explanation a person develops regarding an experience, event, condition, relationship, or object.

Interpretation is influenced by the person's existing perspective and history.

Interpretation is not identical to the thing being interpreted.

It can be:

- more or less accurate;
- more or less complete;
- useful or unhelpful;
- coherent or contradictory;
- and effective or ineffective when used to guide action.

## 5. Shared Reality

Shared reality consists of conditions, events, relationships, objects, practices, and consequences that multiple beings can encounter, affect, communicate about, or participate in.

Shared reality does not require complete agreement.

People may participate in the same event while interpreting it differently.

Agreement can create shared conventions, classifications, expectations, and institutions. It does not automatically establish that every agreed-upon claim is accurate.

**Distinction:** Facts are not identical to agreement. Shared reality can contain both accurate and inaccurate agreed-upon claims.

## 6. Expression

Deliberate human expression begins through internal processes that may include thought, feeling, intuition, memory, imagination, desire, and intention.

Expression occurs when those internal processes are translated into action, communication, design, or participation in the shared world.

Expression does not guarantee that the shared world will change as intended.

Once expressed, an intention encounters conditions that the person does not fully control.

The result creates new information that may require revision of:

- the action;
- the interpretation;
- the desired outcome;
- or the relationship between the person and the outcome.

## 7. Internal Reality

A thought, emotion, intention, fear, image, imagined possibility, or internal conflict is real as an experience occurring within the person.

Its internal reality means that it can influence attention, decisions, behavior, relationships, and expression.

It does not necessarily mean that its interpretation of external conditions is accurate.

THP must respect the reality of an experience without automatically treating every claim arising from it as fact.

## 8. Facts, Truth, Belief, and Agreement

A **fact** is a condition, event, or relationship that is the case regardless of whether it is currently recognized or accepted.

A factual claim is a statement that attempts to describe such a condition and may be supported, contradicted, or revised through evidence.

**Truth** is the quality of a claim, account, or interpretation corresponding adequately to what is the case within its stated scope.

Human access to truth is incomplete and mediated through perception, language, evidence, interpretation, and method.

A **belief** is a proposition a person accepts or treats as true. Beliefs may be accurate or inaccurate, justified or unjustified.

For distinctions among beliefs, desires, values, principles, traits, and styles, see [`THP-VALUES-PRINCIPLES-WILL.md`](./THP-VALUES-PRINCIPLES-WILL.md) (`THP-PH-003`).

**Agreement** is a consolidated or shared response among multiple parties. Agreement may support coordination, but it does not by itself establish truth.

**Meaning** is the relevance, significance, or role attributed to something within a person, relationship, activity, or system.

**Distinction:** Facts are not defined as agreement. Truth is not defined as agreement among entities.

## 9. Accuracy, Usefulness, Effectiveness, and Legitimacy

An interpretation is **accurate** when it describes the relevant conditions and relationships sufficiently well within its stated scope.

An interpretation is **useful** when it assists understanding, decision-making, coordination, efficiency, effectiveness, learning, or another stated purpose.

An action is **effective** when it contributes materially to its intended result.

An action is **legitimate** when the authority and process through which it affects others can be responsibly justified.

Legitimacy requires more than intention or effectiveness. It must consider:

- informed approval or participation where possible;
- transparency of direction;
- the ability to opt in or refuse;
- consequences for affected parties;
- proportionality;
- responsibility;
- and limits imposed by physical, emotional, or psychological harm.

**Distinction:** Effectiveness does not imply legitimacy. An action may work and still be unjustified in how it affects others.

## 10. Intention and Imposition

Sincere intention can still produce harm or imposition.

Intention is therefore necessary for understanding an action but insufficient for legitimizing it.

When a person or institution attempts to reshape shared conditions, THP should examine:

- who is affected;
- who participated in the decision;
- what information was available;
- what alternatives existed;
- whether refusal was possible;
- which harms were foreseeable;
- and who accepts responsibility for the consequences.

## 11. Sovereignty and Responsibility

A person possesses meaningful sovereignty over their interpretation, attention, decisions, expression, and the meaning assigned to voluntary personal sacrifice.

That sovereignty is not unlimited when actions affect other beings.

A person cannot use personal reality alone to determine what is factually true for everyone or to dismiss consequences imposed upon others.

THP must never allow a founder's personal interpretation to override responsibility for decisions and their effects.

**Distinction:** Claiming responsibility is a practice of examining participation and improving action. It is not identical to total causal control over every outcome.

## 12. Body, Mind, Spirit, Self, and Consciousness

The founder currently uses the following **provisional model** for inquiry:

- The **body** is the physical organism through which a person acts and experiences physical conditions.
- The **mind** is the person's cognitive and perceptual relationship with experience, thought, memory, and consciousness.
- The **self** is the changing internal structure involved in translating experience, thought, values, intention, and decision into expression.
- The **spirit** refers provisionally to will, direction, vitality, and the capacity to continue moving intentionally.
- **Consciousness** refers provisionally to the capacity through which experience, awareness, and internal interaction occur.

The metaphysical nature of spirit and consciousness remains unresolved.

THP may study their observable manifestations without claiming that their ultimate nature has been empirically established.

## 13. Governing Position

The Hard Port studies how internal experience and interpretation interact with:

- external conditions;
- attention;
- intention;
- action;
- other people;
- institutional structures;
- and consequences.

It does not treat subjective experience as irrelevant.

It also does not treat subjectivity as sufficient proof of external accuracy.

## 14. Institutional Commitment

THP seeks truth while acknowledging that human access to it is partial and revisable.

Usefulness does not take priority over truth.

However, a currently unhelpful or unusable finding should not automatically be dismissed. The institution may need to change its methods, structures, or questions before its relevance becomes visible.

THP must remain capable of correcting the founder, its researchers, its commercial services, and Tipper when their interpretations fail to account responsibly for observed conditions and consequences.

## 15. Operating Cycle

For SMB research, media observation, and assessment work, THP applies this cycle:

```text
External conditions
        ↓
Experience
        ↓
Interpretation
        ↓
Intention
        ↓
Expression
        ↓
Consequences
        ↓
Revision
```

## 16. Open Questions

The following questions remain open:

1. Is consciousness produced by physical processes, fundamental to reality, or something else?

2. Does spirit identify a distinct phenomenon, or does it combine will, meaning, motivation, and consciousness?

3. How can subjective experience be studied without reducing it to external metrics?

4. How should THP evaluate facts that cannot be directly observed?

5. How should legitimacy be evaluated when full consent is impossible?

6. How should voluntary sacrifice be distinguished from normalized or misunderstood harm?

7. How does personal meaning become shared meaning?

8. When does interpretation become sufficiently supported to qualify as knowledge?

### Founder metaphysics — not institutional facts

Raw founder responses in [`PH-001.md`](../../philosophy/PH-001.md) propose positions THP has **not** adopted as institutional knowledge. These remain open or contested:

- **Consciousness as pure energy** — founder language; metaphysical nature unresolved
- **All existence being created by consciousness** — founder language ("everything is mind"); not THP's institutional position on external existence
- **Spirit as a metaphysical force** — provisional operational language only; ultimate nature unresolved
- **Truth as agreement** — rejected as institutional definition; truth requires correspondence within scope
- **Facts as agreement** — rejected as institutional definition; facts can exist unrecognized
- **Harm existing only when accepted** — rejected as institutional principle; harm limits apply regardless of acceptance

**Distinction:** Founder metaphysics and institutional knowledge are separate layers. Testimony informs inquiry; it does not govern conclusions.
