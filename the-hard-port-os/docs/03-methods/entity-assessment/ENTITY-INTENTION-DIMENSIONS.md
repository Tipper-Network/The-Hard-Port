# Four Dimensions of Entity Intention

> **Status:** `candidate`, `non_canonical`  
> **Philosophical source:** [`THP-PH-002`](../../00-foundations/THP-SELF-INTENTION-EXPRESSION.md) §16

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Definition |
|---|---|
| **stated_intention** | What the entity explicitly claims it wants to accomplish or contribute |
| **governing_intention** | The underlying purpose, priorities, incentives, and commitments that consistently guide decisions |
| **enacted_intention** | The direction revealed through allocation of attention, authority, resources, policies, and repeated actions |
| **perceived_intention** | What affected people and communities understand the entity to be pursuing from their experience |

These four dimensions may align or contradict one another. Contradiction is a primary assessment signal.

## Explicit Non-Mapping

**Do not automatically map** these dimensions one-to-one onto:

- goal
- vision
- mission
- brand

Those terms vary by entity, industry, and governance context. An entity's mission statement may reflect `stated_intention` in some cases and marketing language in others. Brand may express parts of organizational identity without revealing `governing_intention` or `enacted_intention`.

Record the four dimensions directly. Map to goal/vision/mission/brand only when the entity's own documents and governance explicitly define those relationships — and note the mapping as contextual, not automatic.

## Assessment Questions

- Where do stated and enacted intentions diverge?
- What incentives appear to govern decisions regardless of stated purpose?
- What do customers, workers, or communities perceive — and on what evidence?
- Who is authorized to adopt THP's analysis as entity direction?

## Terminology

Use **organizational identity** in operational documents until organizational consciousness is supported as more than a hypothesis ([`HYP-002-organizational-consciousness.md`](../../../research/hypotheses/HYP-002-organizational-consciousness.md)).
