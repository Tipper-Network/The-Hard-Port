# Drift 03 — The Forgotten Customer

## Central thesis

Small businesses are often good at human interaction but poor at retaining the knowledge created by those interactions.

The owner may personally recognize customers while the business has no durable memory of them.

The relationship repeatedly disappears after the transaction.

## Filming objective

Record one continuous discussion about how businesses can be surrounded by customers while behaving as if every sale must begin from zero.

**Target raw recording length:** 20–30 minutes

**Expected output:**

- 3 foundational videos
- 8 short observations
- customer-experience B-roll
- quieter, more human delivery

## Continuous recording prompt

Speak through this question:

How can a business have regular customers and still behave as if it must acquire everyone from the beginning?

Move through:

- first purchases;
- customer recognition;
- loyalty cards;
- discounts;
- private conversations;
- WhatsApp messages;
- names remembered by one employee;
- feedback that disappears;
- customers who quietly stop returning;
- absent follow-up;
- forgotten preferences;
- transactional definitions of loyalty.

## FND-007 — I Noticed Why Loyalty Programs Do Not Create Loyalty

### Primary observation

A loyalty program can reward repeated purchasing.

It does not automatically produce belonging, trust, recognition, or participation.

### Opening options

- "I noticed why loyalty programs do not always create loyalty."
- "Ten stamps can produce a free coffee. They cannot guarantee a relationship."
- "Businesses often reward repetition and call the result loyalty."

### Speaking beats

- The customer receives points, stamps, or discounts.
- The business sees repeated transactions.
- Repetition is interpreted as emotional loyalty.
- The customer may only be responding to convenience or price.
- The business learns little about the person.
- No deeper relationship is created.
- The reward disappears and so may the customer.

### Strong lines

- A repeated purchase is evidence of behavior, not proof of belonging.
- A discount can bring someone back without giving them a reason to care.
- Loyalty is not only what a customer receives. It is whether the relationship continues to mean something.

### Closing options

- "A loyalty program can count visits. It cannot create the reason someone wants to belong."
- "The customer may be loyal to the reward, not the business."
- "Transactions can repeat without the relationship ever developing."

## FND-008 — I Noticed Why Customers Stop Coming Back

### Primary observation

Customers do not always leave because of one dramatic failure.

Many relationships simply fade because the business does nothing to continue them.

### Opening options

- "I noticed why customers stop coming back."
- "Sometimes a customer does not reject a business. The relationship just disappears."
- "The sale ends, the customer leaves, and the business behaves as if nothing worth remembering happened."

### Speaking beats

- The first experience may have been good.
- The customer leaves.
- No meaningful follow-up occurs.
- Their preferences and feedback are not retained.
- The business returns to attracting new people.
- The customer forms a habit elsewhere.
- Silence is interpreted as rejection rather than forgotten continuation.

### Strong lines

- The customer did not always leave. Sometimes the business stopped the relationship at the transaction.
- A good experience can still be forgotten if nothing carries it forward.
- Businesses lose customers quietly long before they notice them missing.

### Closing options

- "The customer may not have chosen someone else. They may simply have formed a relationship where one continued."
- "What looks like customer loss may actually be relationship neglect."
- "The business completed the sale but failed to continue the memory."

## FND-009 — Businesses Think They Need More Customers

### Primary observation

Many businesses already have:

- previous customers;
- occasional visitors;
- people who asked questions;
- people who attended events;
- people who recommended them;
- people who intended to return.

The business treats these people as disconnected moments rather than an existing relationship base.

### Opening options

- "Businesses think they need more customers."
- "Some businesses do not have an acquisition problem. They have a forgetting problem."
- "A business can be surrounded by past customers and still feel like nobody knows it."

### Speaking beats

- New customers feel like growth.
- Existing relationships feel ordinary.
- The business invests in strangers.
- Previous customers receive little attention.
- Feedback and preferences disappear.
- The business repeatedly pays to reacquire attention.
- More people enter while just as many fade out.

### Strong lines

- The business keeps filling the bucket without noticing the bottom is open.
- Customer acquisition becomes expensive when customer memory is weak.
- The forgotten customer is still part of the market. The business simply stopped recognizing them.

### Closing options

- "The business may not need more customers. It may need fewer forgotten customers."
- "Before asking who has never visited, ask what happened to the people who already did."
- "Growth is not only bringing people in. It is developing the ability to continue the relationship."

## Suggested filming structure

### Part 1 — Free drift

Record 15–20 minutes about the difference between repeated transactions and continued relationships.

### Part 2 — Foundational pickups

Record the openings and endings with slower pauses.

### Part 3 — Short observations

Record OBS-017 through OBS-024.

### Part 4 — B-roll

Capture:

- a regular customer arriving;
- a staff member recognizing an order;
- a stamp card;
- a WhatsApp conversation;
- a customer leaving;
- an empty seat;
- feedback written on paper;
- a staff shift changing.

## Delivery direction

- Warmer and quieter than the first two drifts.
- Less sarcasm.
- Let the customer relationship feel human.
- Avoid turning the video into CRM advice.
- Do not explain the solution yet.
