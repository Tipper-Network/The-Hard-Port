# Drift 05 — The Partnership Illusion

## Central thesis

Small businesses want partnerships, but most collaborations begin without a shared objective, clear contribution, customer fit, or method for learning.

Two businesses mentioning each other is not automatically a partnership.

## Filming objective

Record one continuous discussion about why collaborations often produce a temporary post but no lasting business value.

**Target raw recording length:** 15–20 minutes

**Expected output:**

- 1 foundational video
- 7 short observations
- collaboration B-roll
- a bridge toward THP's future partnership thesis

## Continuous recording prompt

Speak through this question:

Why do small-business partnerships feel exciting at the beginning and disappear immediately afterward?

Move through:

- giveaways;
- cross-posting;
- event collaborations;
- exchanging discounts;
- vague promises of exposure;
- mismatched audiences;
- unclear responsibilities;
- no measurement;
- no shared customer problem;
- no follow-up;
- partnerships based on friendship rather than fit.

Establish the problem clearly.

## FND-012 — I Noticed Why Partnerships Between Small Businesses Fail

### Primary observation

Many partnerships begin with enthusiasm rather than structure.

The businesses do not clearly define:

- the customer problem;
- the shared objective;
- what each side contributes;
- who is responsible for what;
- what success means;
- what should continue afterward.

### Opening options

- "I noticed why partnerships between small businesses fail."
- "Two businesses posting each other's logos is not necessarily a partnership."
- "Most collaborations begin with excitement and end with one post nobody learns from."

### Speaking beats

- Two owners like each other.
- They decide to collaborate.
- The idea is described as exposure.
- Contributions remain vague.
- The audiences may not meaningfully overlap.
- No clear result is defined.
- The collaboration happens once.
- Neither business knows whether it worked.
- Nothing durable remains.

### Strong lines

- Two businesses mentioning each other is not a partnership.
- Exposure is not a shared objective.
- A partnership should leave both businesses more capable than before.
- Most collaborations exchange attention without creating a relationship between the businesses.

### Closing options

- "A partnership is not the moment two businesses appear together. It is the structure that allows them to create value together."
- "If neither business can explain what became possible because of the other, the collaboration may have been content, not partnership."
- "The post disappeared because nothing beneath it was designed to continue."
