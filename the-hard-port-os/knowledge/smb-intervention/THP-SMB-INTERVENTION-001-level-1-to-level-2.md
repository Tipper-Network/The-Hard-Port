# Level 1-to-Level 2 Intervention System

> **Status:** `working` · `development_state: ready_for_lifecycle_design` · `field_validation: pending_first_entity`  
> **Not canonical.** Detailed exercises and thresholds validated against first real entity.  
> **Official phase order:** Business Reality → Evidence Recovery → Viable Core → Constraint Diagnosis → Identity Clarification → Relationship Foundation → Operating Foundation → Level 2 Proof

## Lifecycle Position

The intervention begins only after:

- Qualification
- Diagnosis
- Findings presentation
- Mutual engagement decision
- Agreement
- Required consent
- Initial payment condition
- Kickoff

See [THP SMB Client Engagement Lifecycle](../smb-engagement/THP-SMB-ENGAGEMENT-001-client-lifecycle.md) — Stages 11–13 (Intervention Execution, Progress Review, Level 2 Reassessment); Stage 14 (Completion and Handoff).

The intervention ends through Level 2 confirmation, partial completion, continuation, pause, referral, withdrawal, or termination.

## Evidence and Data Boundary

Engagement evidence must be classified according to:

[`THP-SMB-RESEARCH-001-evidence-data-consent-system.md`](../smb-research/THP-SMB-RESEARCH-001-evidence-data-consent-system.md)

Institutional Learning Records do not automatically authorize anonymized research, case studies, public content, or Tipper use.

## 1. Purpose

The Level 1-to-Level 2 Intervention helps an established but survival-driven microbusiness build the foundation required for stable and deliberate operation.

The intervention is intended to move the business from:

> Repeated reactive activity without reliable interpretation

toward:

> A sufficiently clear, observable, and repeatable business foundation that can prepare for responsible growth.

THP does not promise that every business will reach Level 2.

THP promises to:

* Establish what is actually happening.
* Organize available evidence.
* Identify the viable core.
* Identify the primary barrier to stability.
* Help the business make and implement deliberate decisions.
* Establish the systems required to measure progress.
* Determine honestly whether Level 2 has been reached.

---

## 2. Intervention Position

The intervention begins after:

1. Engagement Readiness Application.
2. First-Onboarder Qualification.
3. Initial Level 1 Diagnostic.
4. Findings presentation.
5. Founder agreement to proceed.

The intervention ends with:

* Level 2 confirmation.
* Partial completion and continuation.
* Return to an earlier intervention phase.
* Referral to another specialist.
* Pause.
* Termination.

The full engagement sequence is:

> Application → Qualification → Diagnostic → Findings → Intervention → Reassessment

## 3. Intervention Doctrine

## 3.1 Diagnose before producing

THP does not begin with content calendars, rebranding, paid promotion, or campaign production.

Execution begins only after THP understands which business condition the work is intended to change.

## 3.2 Preserve evidence before changing the business

Existing customer behavior, messages, transactions, offers, content, and operating practices contain evidence.

THP should recover and interpret that evidence before introducing major changes.

## 3.3 Protect the viable core

The intervention must identify what customers already value and avoid destroying it through unnecessary repositioning or expansion.

## 3.4 Address the primary constraint

THP does not attempt to fix every weakness simultaneously.

The intervention identifies the condition most responsible for preventing Level 2 and organizes the work around it.

## 3.5 Separate decisions from deliverables

A logo, content plan, customer interview, dashboard, or workflow is not progress by itself.

Each deliverable must support a defined decision, behavior, or Level 2 gate.

## 3.6 Founder participation is mandatory

THP can structure, investigate, interpret, recommend, and support implementation.

The founder remains responsible for:

* Providing evidence.
* Making business decisions.
* Participating in customer learning.
* Implementing agreed operational changes.
* Disclosing material constraints.
* Reviewing results honestly.

## 3.7 Stabilization comes before growth

The intervention must not be converted into an acquisition or scaling campaign before the Level 2 foundation is sufficiently established.

## 3.8 Evidence determines completion

The intervention is not complete because the planned deliverables were produced.

It is complete when the relevant Level 2 conditions are supported by evidence.

## 4. Entry Conditions

The business may enter the intervention when:

* It has passed the Qualification Gate.
* Its Level 1 classification is sufficiently supported.
* The founder has received and understood the diagnostic findings.
* The founder accepts that THP is not guaranteeing growth.
* The founder agrees to provide evidence and participate.
* Immediate legal, financial, ownership, safety, or technical crises do not block the work.
* The business remains actively capable of delivering its core offer.
* THP and the founder have agreed on the initial intervention boundary.

Before work begins, THP should record:

* Current maturity classification.
* Strongest and weakest dimensions.
* Primary suspected constraint.
* Immediate risks.
* Existing evidence.
* Evidence gaps.
* Founder expectations.
* Conditions requiring pause or referral.

## 5. Phase 1 — Business Reality

## Purpose

Establish a shared, evidence-based description of what the business currently is.

This phase separates:

* What the founder intended to build.
* What the business says publicly.
* What it currently sells.
* What customers actually buy.
* How the business currently operates.

## Central Question

> What business exists in practice today?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Map the current offers.
* Map the active customer journey.
* Map revenue-producing activities.
* Identify active channels.
* Document the founder’s current role.
* Identify current team responsibilities.
* Record the business’s major recurring activities.
* Separate current reality from future ambitions.
* Identify major contradictions between claims and evidence.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Explain the business without using aspirational language.
* Provide existing offers, prices, and customer examples.
* Describe how customers discover, purchase, receive, and respond.
* Identify current responsibilities.
* Disclose recent failures, interruptions, and operational pressures.
* Confirm which activities are active, paused, or abandoned.

## Evidence Produced

* Current-business map.
* Offer inventory.
* Customer-cycle map.
* Revenue-activity map.
* Founder-dependency map.
* Channel inventory.
* Reality-versus-aspiration findings.
* Initial contradiction register.

## Level 2 Gates Addressed

* Customer clarity.
* Financial visibility.
* Operational consistency.
* Founder dependency.
* Evidence and learning.
* Identity clarity.

## Exit Conditions

Phase 1 is complete when THP and the founder can agree on:

* What the business currently sells.
* Who currently buys.
* How value is delivered.
* Where money enters.
* Where the business repeatedly becomes unstable.
* What remains unknown.

The founder does not need to agree with every interpretation.

They must agree that the documented evidence is a fair representation of current reality.

## 6. Phase 2 — Evidence Recovery

## Purpose

Recover, organize, and classify the evidence already produced by the business.

Level 1 businesses often possess useful evidence but leave it fragmented across:

* Direct messages.
* Invoices.
* Social platforms.
* Memory.
* Spreadsheets.
* Customer conversations.
* Reviews.
* Order systems.
* Team chat.
* Personal accounts.

## Central Question

> What has the business already learned without realizing it?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Create an evidence inventory.
* Gather available customer, transaction, communication, and operational records.
* Organize evidence by business cycle.
* Distinguish facts, claims, assumptions, and interpretations.
* Identify repeated customer language.
* Identify repeat, referral, complaint, and disengagement patterns.
* Reconstruct major past business experiments.
* Record missing or unreliable evidence.
* Establish a simple evidence repository.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Provide or reconstruct relevant records.
* Explain the context behind major transactions and decisions.
* Identify changes made to offers, prices, content, or operations.
* Provide customer messages with appropriate privacy protection.
* Identify records that do not exist.
* Stop treating unavailable evidence as proof of a preferred conclusion.

## Evidence Produced

* Evidence inventory.
* Customer-evidence repository.
* Transaction summary.
* Offer-performance history.
* Content and promotion history.
* Previous-experiment register.
* Assumption register.
* Evidence-gap register.
* Evidence-status classification.

## Level 2 Gates Addressed

* Demand clarity.
* Customer clarity.
* Retention and community evidence.
* Financial visibility.
* Evidence and learning.
* Identity clarity.

## Exit Conditions

Phase 2 is complete when:

* Enough evidence exists to compare the business’s primary offers and customer patterns.
* Important claims have evidence statuses.
* Major evidence gaps are visible.
* THP can distinguish known conditions from assumptions.
* The business has a usable location for future evidence.

## 7. Phase 3 — Viable Core

## Purpose

Identify the smallest coherent part of the business that demonstrates meaningful value and deserves protection.

The viable core is not automatically:

* The founder’s favorite offer.
* The highest-priced offer.
* The most popular content.
* The offer with the most sales.
* The activity generating the most attention.

It is the strongest current relationship between:

* A customer group.
* A meaningful need or desire.
* An offer.
* Delivered value.
* Repeat or continued behavior.
* Sustainable business participation.

## Central Question

> What part of this business is worth stabilizing?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Compare current offers.
* Identify recurring demand patterns.
* Identify the most meaningful customer group.
* Analyze reasons for purchase.
* Analyze reasons for return, referral, disengagement, and complaint.
* Identify value that customers consistently recognize.
* Compare commercial value with operational burden.
* Identify which relationships the business should protect.
* Draft the viable-core definition.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Participate in customer review.
* Separate personal preference from customer evidence.
* Identify activities the business may need to reduce or stop.
* Confirm operational realities.
* Speak directly with customers when evidence is insufficient.
* Decide which core offer and customer group deserve priority.

## Evidence Produced

* Core-customer definition.
* Core-offer definition.
* Delivered-value statement.
* Reasons-to-buy map.
* Reasons-to-return map.
* Reasons-to-leave map.
* Core-relationship list.
* Offer-priority decision.
* Stop, continue, investigate register.

## Level 2 Gates Addressed

* Demand clarity.
* Customer clarity.
* Retention and community evidence.
* Financial visibility.
* Identity clarity.

## Exit Conditions

Phase 3 is complete when the business can state:

* Who the current core customer is.
* What they are trying to accomplish.
* What they buy.
* Why they choose the business.
* What value they experience.
* What causes them to return or remain connected.
* Which offer and relationship should be protected.
* Which major assumptions remain unproven.

---

## 8. Phase 4 — Constraint Diagnosis

## Purpose

Determine the primary condition preventing the viable core from becoming stable.

The constraint is not automatically “marketing.”

Possible constraints include:

* Weak demand.
* Poor customer fit.
* Unclear value.
* Low retention.
* Unsustainable pricing.
* Weak margins.
* Inconsistent delivery.
* Limited capacity.
* Founder dependency.
* Unstructured decision-making.
* Identity confusion.
* Fragmented customer relationships.
* Lack of usable evidence.

## Central Question

> What most prevents the viable core from becoming repeatable and stable?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Compare gaps across dimensions A–H.
* Identify causal relationships between problems.
* Separate symptoms from constraints.
* Identify which condition controls the others.
* Test the suspected constraint against available evidence.
* Determine what should not be addressed yet.
* Define the intervention priority.
* Establish baseline indicators.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Review the causal explanation.
* Challenge unsupported interpretations.
* Confirm practical constraints.
* Select the primary intervention priority.
* Accept that some visible problems will remain temporarily untreated.
* Stop launching unrelated corrective actions during the intervention.

## Evidence Produced

* Primary-constraint statement.
* Secondary-constraint list.
* Symptom-versus-cause map.
* Baseline indicators.
* Intervention-priority decision.
* Deferred-problem register.
* Initial success and failure conditions.

## Level 2 Gates Addressed

Potentially all Level 2 gates, depending on the identified constraint.

## Exit Conditions

Phase 4 is complete when:

* One primary constraint has been selected.
* Evidence supporting the selection is recorded.
* Important alternative explanations are documented.
* THP and the founder know what will be addressed first.
* They also know what will not be addressed yet.
* Baseline conditions are recorded.

---

## 9. Phase 5 — Identity Clarification

## Purpose

Translate the viable core into a coherent business identity and communication foundation.

Identity is not treated as visual decoration.

It should explain:

* What the business does.
* For whom.
* Why it matters.
* What it consistently values.
* What customers should expect.
* What role it occupies within its community.

## Central Question

> How should the business express the value it can genuinely deliver?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Clarify the value proposition.
* Define the core customer-facing promise.
* Identify supporting proof.
* Clarify business principles.
* Identify language already used by customers.
* Define communication priorities.
* Identify misleading, generic, or unsupported claims.
* Establish message hierarchy.
* Determine whether visual or brand changes are actually required.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Confirm what the business can reliably promise.
* Remove language that is aspirational but unsupported.
* Separate personal identity from business identity.
* Approve the communication hierarchy.
* Commit to consistent use of the clarified identity.
* Participate in any required brand or message implementation.

## Evidence Produced

* Business-definition statement.
* Core value proposition.
* Customer promise.
* Supporting proof points.
* Message hierarchy.
* Business principles.
* Communication boundaries.
* Identity-preservation rules.
* Brand or content implementation brief, where necessary.

## Level 2 Gates Addressed

* Customer clarity.
* Identity clarity.
* Demand clarity.
* Founder dependency.
* Evidence and learning.

## Exit Conditions

Phase 5 is complete when:

* The business can clearly explain what it provides, for whom, and why it matters.
* Its claims are supported by actual evidence or clearly labeled hypotheses.
* The communication hierarchy is usable.
* The founder and business identities are sufficiently distinguished.
* Any branding or content work has a defined strategic purpose.

---

## 10. Phase 6 — Relationship Foundation

## Purpose

Turn disconnected transactions and scattered feedback into observable customer relationships.

This phase establishes the early foundation for:

* Retention.
* Referrals.
* Participation.
* Feedback.
* Community stability.

## Central Question

> How will the business remain connected to the people who value it?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Identify core supporters and repeat customers.
* Map current relationship touchpoints.
* Establish a consistent feedback method.
* Define post-delivery follow-up.
* Create a repeat and referral observation system.
* Identify opportunities for meaningful customer participation.
* Distinguish audience engagement from customer and community behavior.
* Determine which relationship functions may later belong in Tipper.
* Keep the initial process manual when necessary.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Participate in customer conversations.
* Follow up after delivery.
* Invite honest feedback.
* Record repeat, referral, complaint, and disengagement behavior.
* Stop using public engagement as a substitute for customer understanding.
* Respond to community evidence without defensiveness.
* Test simple participation mechanisms.

## Evidence Produced

* Core-supporter register.
* Feedback method.
* Post-delivery follow-up process.
* Repeat and referral record.
* Customer-question register.
* Participation observations.
* Community-pattern findings.
* Manual Tipper-requirement map.

## Level 2 Gates Addressed

* Customer clarity.
* Retention and community evidence.
* Evidence and learning.
* Identity clarity.
* Demand clarity.

## Exit Conditions

Phase 6 is complete when:

* The business can identify its core supporters.
* A repeatable feedback process exists.
* Post-delivery behavior is being observed.
* Repeat and referral patterns can be recorded.
* The business has at least one meaningful participation or relationship mechanism.
* Community is defined through behavior rather than follower count.

---

## 11. Phase 7 — Operating Foundation

## Purpose

Create the minimum operational rhythm required to deliver the viable core consistently and make deliberate decisions.

This phase does not attempt to optimize the entire business.

It stabilizes the activities most necessary for Level 2.

## Central Question

> What must become repeatable for the viable core to remain dependable?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Map the core delivery workflow.
* Identify major bottlenecks.
* Define minimum quality standards.
* Clarify responsibilities.
* Externalize essential founder knowledge.
* Establish capacity boundaries.
* Define recurring evidence reviews.
* Create a basic decision rhythm.
* Identify tasks suitable for delegation.
* Connect communication activity to business priorities.
* Establish operational stop conditions.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Document core routines.
* Assign responsibilities.
* Protect strategic review time.
* Delegate selected work.
* Record failures and exceptions.
* Respect capacity limits.
* Stop accepting work that damages the viable core.
* Use the agreed decision and evidence rhythm.

## Evidence Produced

* Core workflow.
* Responsibility map.
* Minimum quality standard.
* Capacity boundary.
* Bottleneck register.
* Founder-knowledge externalization.
* Review rhythm.
* Decision record.
* Operating checklist.
* Content and communication operating rhythm, where required.

## Level 2 Gates Addressed

* Operational consistency.
* Founder dependency.
* Financial visibility.
* Evidence and learning.
* Identity clarity.

## Exit Conditions

Phase 7 is complete when:

* The core offer can be delivered through a repeatable workflow.
* Responsibilities are visible.
* Basic quality standards exist.
* Capacity is understood.
* Essential information exists outside the founder’s memory.
* The business has a recurring review and decision rhythm.
* Moderate demand no longer immediately produces complete disorder.

## 12. Phase 8 — Level 2 Proof

**Proof system:** [`THP-SMB-PROOF-001`](../smb-proof/THP-SMB-PROOF-001-case-study-and-proof-system.md) · [`THP-SMB-TEMPLATE-006`](../smb-proof/templates/THP-SMB-TEMPLATE-006-proof-record.md). Completion is based on Level 2 gate evidence — not deliverable completion alone.

## Purpose

Determine whether the business has established the Level 2 foundation.

This phase is not a celebratory review of completed deliverables.

It is an evidence-based maturity reassessment.

## Central Question

> Has the business moved beyond immediate survival and become stable enough to prepare for growth?

## THP Actions

THP should:

* Reassess all Level 2 gates.
* Compare current evidence with the original baseline.
* Review stability across completed business cycles.
* Identify remaining weak dimensions.
* Test whether the viable core remains functional.
* Review founder dependency.
* Review financial visibility.
* Review customer and community evidence.
* Review operational consistency.
* Determine the final transition status.
* Produce the intervention conclusion.

## Founder Actions

The founder should:

* Provide current evidence.
* Review the findings.
* Explain major changes and exceptions.
* Identify where new routines are not being followed.
* Confirm which decisions have become normal practice.
* Decide whether to consolidate, continue, pause, or prepare for Level 3.

## Evidence Produced

* Level 2 reassessment.
* Baseline comparison.
* Gate-by-gate conclusion.
* Remaining-risk register.
* Founder-capability assessment.
* Transition decision.
* Continuation or completion plan.
* Candidate case-study evidence.
* Institutional learning record.

## Level 2 Gates Assessed

All Level 2 gates:

* Demand clarity.
* Customer clarity.
* Retention and community evidence.
* Financial visibility.
* Operational consistency.
* Reduced founder reactivity.
* Evidence and learning.
* Identity clarity.

## Exit Outcomes

### Level 2 Confirmed

Use when all mandatory Level 2 gates are sufficiently supported and stability has been demonstrated across the required cycles.

### Level 2 Foundation Partially Established

Use when meaningful progress exists but one or more mandatory gates remain unsupported.

The engagement may continue with a revised intervention priority.

### Return to Earlier Phase

Use when new evidence invalidates a previous conclusion.

### Referred or Paused

Use when another professional, crisis, or external dependency must be addressed.

### Intervention Terminated

Use when the founder or business no longer meets the participation or evidence conditions.

## 14. Execution Services

THP may provide or coordinate execution services when they are required by the intervention.

Examples include:

* Brand identity work.
* Messaging.
* Website or profile updates.
* Content strategy.
* Content production.
* Customer research.
* Feedback implementation.
* Operational documentation.
* Workflow design.
* Community communication.
* Basic reporting systems.

Execution services must reference:

* The constraint being addressed.
* The decision authorizing the work.
* The intended behavior or outcome.
* The evidence used to evaluate it.

Execution must not become an independent stream of activity disconnected from the intervention.

## 15. Decision Points

Formal decisions should be recorded after:

1. Business Reality.
2. Evidence Recovery.
3. Viable Core.
4. Constraint Diagnosis.
5. Identity Clarification.
6. Relationship Foundation.
7. Operating Foundation.
8. Level 2 Reassessment.

Each decision should record:

* Evidence reviewed.
* Finding.
* Alternatives considered.
* Founder decision.
* THP recommendation.
* Action.
* Responsible person.
* Review point.

---

## 16. Pause Conditions

THP may pause the intervention when:

* Required evidence has not been provided.
* The founder is temporarily unable to participate.
* The business enters an immediate legal, financial, ownership, safety, or technical crisis.
* The business cannot currently deliver its core offer.
* A critical external dependency blocks progress.
* The founder begins making major uncoordinated changes that invalidate the intervention.
* Continuing would produce unreliable conclusions.

A pause should record:

* Reason.
* Conditions for resumption.
* Evidence required.
* Responsible party.
* Review date or trigger.

---

## 17. Referral Conditions

THP should refer the business when it requires work outside THP’s present competence, including:

* Licensed legal advice.
* Accounting or tax intervention.
* Debt restructuring.
* Clinical or mental-health support.
* Product safety assessment.
* Specialized technical remediation.
* Ownership mediation.
* Emergency financing.
* Regulatory approval.

Referral does not automatically end the THP relationship.

The intervention may resume after the blocking condition is addressed.

---

## 18. Termination Conditions

THP may terminate the intervention when:

* The founder repeatedly refuses to provide agreed evidence.
* Business information is deliberately misrepresented.
* The founder refuses all contradictory evidence.
* THP is expected to guarantee commercial outcomes.
* The founder repeatedly ignores agreed capacity, safety, or survival boundaries.
* The business uses THP’s work deceptively or unlawfully.
* The founder becomes unable or unwilling to participate.
* Continued intervention would not be professionally responsible.
* Payment or contractual obligations remain unresolved under the future engagement agreement.

Termination should record:

* Reason.
* Evidence.
* Previous correction attempts.
* Final obligations.
* Whether reapplication is possible.

---

## 19. Completion Conditions

The intervention is complete when:

* Level 2 has been confirmed, or
* THP and the founder conclude that the Level 2 transition cannot responsibly continue under current conditions.

Completion requires:

* Final reassessment.
* Remaining-risk register.
* Founder review.
* Evidence archive.
* Institutional learning record.
* Next-stage recommendation.

Possible recommendations include:

* Consolidate Level 2.
* Begin Level 3 preparation.
* Continue a focused stability intervention.
* Seek an external specialist.
* Pause the business.
* Redesign or discontinue part of the offer.
* End the THP engagement.

---

## 20. Institutional Learning

Every intervention should produce a separate THP learning record containing:

* Starting assumptions.
* Initial classification.
* Evidence availability.
* Primary constraint.
* Intervention decisions.
* Founder response.
* Customer and community evidence.
* Outcomes.
* Failed actions.
* Unexpected findings.
* Maturity-model implications.
* Diagnostic improvements.
* Service-methodology improvements.
* Potential Tipper requirements.
* Potential content insights.

Client-sensitive information must not be converted into institutional publishing without the appropriate consent and anonymization structure.

---
