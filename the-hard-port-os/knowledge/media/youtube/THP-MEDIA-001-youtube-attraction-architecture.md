# THP YouTube Attraction Architecture

> **Status:** `working` · **Not canonical.**  
> `field_validation: pending_first_12_video_test` · `application_route: pending_operational_setup`

## Status

Working media architecture.

Ready for the first 12-video field test. Titles, formats, and calls to action should be refined using viewer responses and first-entity evidence.

---

# 1. Channel Purpose

The Hard Port uses media to:

1. Make survival-mode business conditions visible.
2. Challenge false explanations for those conditions.
3. Give owners a more accurate way to understand their businesses.
4. Explain the transition from Level 1 survival to Level 2 stability.
5. Attract businesses ready for examination, not merely content production.
6. Generate language, objections, and evidence that improve THP.
7. Route suitable owners toward the Engagement Readiness Application.

The channel is the public entrance to the THP institutional loop.

It is not separate from the consulting and research system.

---

# 2. Channel Thesis

> The Hard Port investigates why established microbusinesses remain trapped in survival mode and builds the systems that help them become stable enough to grow responsibly.

## Public-facing version

> Your business may not have a content problem. It may have a clarity, evidence, relationship, or operating problem that content cannot solve.

## Channel promise

> We will help you see the business underneath the posts.

---

# 3. Target Viewer

The primary viewer is:

* The founder of an established microbusiness.
* Already selling to real customers.
* Operating online or depending materially on online communication.
* Still personally involved in most business activity.
* Experiencing inconsistent sales, unclear demand, weak retention, operational pressure, or identity confusion.
* Already attempting content, trends, boosting, branding, promotions, or hiring.
* Beginning to suspect that the problem is deeper than visibility.
* Trying to move from survival toward stability.
* Willing to examine the business rather than only blame the algorithm.

## What this owner says

* "I am posting, but it is not translating into sales."
* "Some people love the business, but I cannot make it predictable."
* "I do not know whether the offer, marketing, or business model is wrong."
* "I have tried everything."
* "I need to grow, but I am already overwhelmed."
* "People engage, but they do not buy."
* "Customers buy once and disappear."
* "The business depends on me for everything."
* "Maybe this business is just not working."

## What this owner needs to discover

* Attention is not the same as demand.
* Followers are not necessarily customers.
* Customers are not automatically a community.
* Branding cannot repair an unclear viable core.
* Content cannot compensate indefinitely for weak retention or operations.
* Growth is dangerous before stability.
* Existing customer behavior contains more information than the owner is currently using.
* The business may be diagnosable before it is condemnable.

---

# 4. Viewers THP Is Not Primarily Targeting

The initial channel is not built for:

* Pre-launch founders with no paying customers.
* People looking for general entrepreneurship motivation.
* Creators seeking only audience-growth tactics.
* Established companies already preparing to scale.
* Businesses seeking guaranteed leads or virality.
* Founders unwilling to examine customer evidence.
* Marketing professionals looking only for content tips.
* Viewers seeking a done-for-you posting service without business investigation.

These people may still watch.

The content should not reorganize itself around them.

---

# 5. Viewer Psychological Progression

The channel must move the owner through six stages.

## Stage 1 — Recognition

The viewer says:

> "That is exactly what is happening to me."

Content exposes recognizable symptoms without immediately selling THP.

## Stage 2 — Disconfirmation

The viewer says:

> "Maybe the explanation I have been using is incomplete."

Content separates:

* Visibility from demand.
* Engagement from customer behavior.
* Branding from business identity.
* Activity from progress.
* Growth from increased workload.

## Stage 3 — Interpretation

The viewer says:

> "There is a system underneath these problems."

THP introduces:

* Business cycles.
* Viable core.
* Evidence.
* Constraints.
* Customer relationships.
* Founder dependency.
* Stability gates.

## Stage 4 — Possibility

The viewer says:

> "I may not need to abandon the business. I may need to understand and stabilize it."

The content introduces the Level 1-to-Level 2 transition.

## Stage 5 — Trust

The viewer says:

> "THP is not simply trying to sell me more marketing activity."

Trust comes from:

* Clear boundaries.
* Honest uncertainty.
* Refusal to promise virality.
* Useful distinctions.
* Evidence.
* Field learning.
* Willingness to state when THP is not the right fit.

## Stage 6 — Readiness

The viewer says:

> "I am willing to let someone examine what is actually happening."

Only then should the application become the primary call to action.

# 6. Content Architecture

THP should publish through five connected content categories.

## Category 1 — Recognition

### Purpose

Help Level 1 owners recognize their condition.

### Questions answered

* What does survival mode look like?
* Why does the business feel busy but remain unstable?
* Why do repeated marketing activities stop producing clarity?
* Why does the founder feel personally trapped inside the business?

### Typical titles

* Why Posting Consistently Is Not Fixing Your Business
* You Are Busy, but Your Business Is Not Becoming Stable
* The Business Owner Who Has Tried Everything
* Your Business Should Not Need a Miracle Every Month

### Primary viewer response

> "This is describing me."

### Call to action

A comment, self-observation, or return for the next video.

Do not immediately push every recognition viewer into an application.

---

## Category 2 — Interpretation

### Purpose

Replace misleading explanations with structural ones.

### Questions answered

* Is the problem really the algorithm?
* Is attention producing demand?
* Does the business understand why customers buy?
* Are followers, customers, and community being confused?
* Is branding being used to avoid a business question?

### Typical titles

* You May Not Have a Content Problem
* Attention Is Not Demand
* Why Branding Cannot Fix an Unclear Business
* Followers, Customers, and Community Are Not the Same
* Your Metrics May Be Hiding the Actual Problem

### Primary viewer response

> "I have been measuring the wrong thing."

### Call to action

Ask the viewer to identify one business assumption or observe one customer behavior.

---

## Category 3 — Transition

### Purpose

Explain the movement from Level 1 survival to Level 2 stability.

### Questions answered

* What must become clear?
* What must become repeatable?
* What should the business protect?
* What evidence is required?
* What should happen before attempting growth?

### Typical titles

* What Must Be True Before Your Business Tries to Grow
* The Difference Between Survival and Stability
* How to Find the Part of Your Business Worth Protecting
* The Systems a Small Business Needs Before Growth
* Level 1 to Level 2: The Real Business Transition

### Primary viewer response

> "I can see a path that is more serious than posting more."

### Call to action

Read the readiness criteria or complete the application when the fit is explicit.

---

## Category 4 — Institutional Thesis

### Purpose

Introduce the wider THP and Tipper worldview.

### Questions answered

* What is a business as a relationship system?
* Why does community matter?
* Why are reviews a weak form of feedback?
* How should businesses and customers learn together?
* Why does THP study business transformation?

### Typical titles

* A Business Is a Relationship System
* Small Businesses Need More Than Customers
* Reviews Are Not Feedback
* Community Is Infrastructure, Not an Audience
* Why THP Is Studying Businesses in Survival Mode

### Primary viewer response

> "THP sees the business as part of a larger social system."

### Call to action

Subscribe, return, participate in a question, or follow the institution's field work.

These videos should not force Tipper promotion before evidence exists.

---

## Category 5 — Proof and Field Learning

### Purpose

Show what THP is observing, testing, learning, changing, and refusing to overclaim.

### Questions answered

* What happened with a real entity?
* Which assumptions failed?
* Which evidence was unavailable?
* What did the intervention reveal?
* What did THP change in its own methodology?

### Typical titles

* What We Learned From the First THP Business
* The Question Our First Diagnostic Got Wrong
* This Business Did Not Need More Content
* What Changed—and What Did Not
* A Failed Intervention Decision and What It Taught Us

### Primary viewer response

> "THP is building from evidence rather than performing expertise."

### Call to action

Apply, follow the next field cycle, or contribute a relevant business experience.

This category begins only when real field evidence exists and the required permissions are recorded.

# 8. Video Formats

## Format A — Focus Essay

A single business belief is challenged and replaced with a stronger interpretation.

Working structure:

1. State the familiar belief.
2. Show why it appears reasonable.
3. Expose the contradiction.
4. Introduce the structural interpretation.
5. Show what the owner should observe.
6. State what the interpretation does not prove.
7. Give one next action.

## Format B — Diagnostic Breakdown

A recognizable business situation is separated into dimensions, evidence, symptoms, and suspected constraints.

Working structure:

1. Describe the visible problem.
2. List the common explanation.
3. Map the business system underneath it.
4. Separate evidence from assumptions.
5. Identify possible constraints.
6. Explain what must be verified.
7. Clarify what should not be done yet.

## Format C — Kinetic Thesis

A short, typography-driven argument built around one strong distinction.

Useful distinctions include:

* Attention versus demand.
* Audience versus community.
* Activity versus learning.
* Branding versus identity.
* Survival versus stability.
* Growth versus increased workload.
* Reviews versus feedback.

This format fits THP's grid, systems, mapping, and kinetic-typography visual language.

## Format D — Founder Reality

Direct narration about what the founder experiences internally and operationally.

The tone should be serious without pity, motivational clichés, or empty reassurance.

## Format E — Port Report

A field note containing:

* What THP expected.
* What happened.
* What evidence appeared.
* What contradicted the expectation.
* What changed in the system.
* What remains uncertain.

# 9. Standard Video Architecture

Every substantial THP video should contain seven elements.

## 1. Recognition hook

Begin with a condition the target owner has experienced.

Example:

> You posted consistently. You followed trends. You boosted. You redesigned the brand. And now you are beginning to wonder whether the business itself is the problem.

## 2. Familiar explanation

State what the owner currently believes.

## 3. Contradiction

Show why that explanation does not account for the observed business condition.

## 4. Structural reframe

Introduce the more useful interpretation.

## 5. Observable evidence

Give the viewer something specific to inspect inside their business.

## 6. Boundary

State what the video cannot establish without examining the business.

## 7. Next action

Use one relevant call to action.

Do not include several unrelated calls to action in the same video.

---

# 10. Call-to-Action Ladder

## CTA Level 1 — Recognition

Used early.

Examples:

* "What have you already tried?"
* "Which part of this describes your business?"
* "Write down where your last three customers came from."

Purpose:

Generate viewer language and self-observation.

## CTA Level 2 — Evidence

Used in interpretation videos.

Examples:

* "Look at your last five customers and record why they bought."
* "Separate the people who engaged from the people who paid."
* "Identify one business assumption you have never verified."

Purpose:

Move the viewer from passive agreement into observation.

## CTA Level 3 — Readiness

Used in transition videos.

Examples:

* "Read the Level 1 conditions."
* "Review what THP requires before diagnosis."
* "Decide whether you are ready to provide evidence."

Purpose:

Allow self-selection before application.

## CTA Level 4 — Application

Used only when the video clearly identifies the business THP accepts.

Example:

> If you operate an established online or online-dependent microbusiness, have served real customers, and are ready to examine what is preventing stability, the THP Engagement Readiness Application is available below.

Purpose:

Route suitable viewers into the intake operation.

## CTA Level 5 — Field participation

Used after real entities exist.

Examples:

* Follow the field cycle.
* Submit a relevant observation.
* Apply for a later validation cycle.
* Participate in a community question.

# 11. Viewer-to-Application Journey

The minimum journey should be:

> YouTube video → THP service explanation → Engagement Readiness Application → Application review → Qualification

## Video description

Each relevant video should contain:

* One-sentence video thesis.
* Who the video is for.
* A link to the public THP explanation.
* The application link only when appropriate.
* A clear statement that applying does not guarantee acceptance.

## Pinned comment

Use one prompt only:

* Observation question, or
* Application route.

Do not use both unless the video is specifically an invitation video.

## Public service explanation

The linked page should explain:

* Who THP helps.
* What Level 1 survival means.
* What THP investigates.
* What the paid diagnostic is.
* What THP does not promise.
* What happens after applying.
* That only one active first-entity engagement may be accepted during field validation.

## Application feedback loop

Every application should record:

* Which video led to the application.
* Which sentence or idea felt most accurate.
* What the applicant believes is wrong.
* What they have already tried.
* What result they expect.

This allows THP to measure whether the media is attracting the intended owner.

See [`THP-ENGAGEMENT-READINESS-APPLICATION.md`](../../docs/03-methods/entity-assessment/THP-ENGAGEMENT-READINESS-APPLICATION.md).

# 14. Publishing Rhythm

Recommended initial rhythm:

* One principal video per week.
* One or two short kinetic excerpts derived from the same thesis.
* One audience question connected to the video's learning objective.
* One review after every three principal videos.
* One complete architectural review after the 12-video test.

Do not create a separate daily-content machine.

Each principal video should produce:

* One institutional thesis.
* One viewer observation.
* One language-learning question.
* One optional short-form cutdown.
* One record of what THP learned.

# 16. Metrics

## Primary metrics

* Qualified applications by source video.
* Percentage of applicants matching the Level 1 entry gate.
* Percentage demonstrating founder readiness.
* Recognition comments from real business owners.
* Repeated viewer language that matches the first-onboarder model.
* Application completion.
* Qualification completion.
* Viewers returning across the series.
* Evidence that viewers performed the requested observation.

## Diagnostic media metrics

* Which belief produced the strongest disagreement?
* Which distinction was misunderstood?
* Which video attracted pre-business applicants?
* Which content attracted marketers instead of business owners?
* Which title attracted viewers expecting generic content advice?
* Which explanation caused the right owner to reveal useful evidence?

## Secondary metrics

* Views.
* Watch time.
* Subscribers.
* Click-through rate.
* Short-form reach.

These matter operationally, but they do not prove THP is attracting the correct entity.

## Failure signals

* Most applicants have no paying customers.
* Applications ask only for content production.
* Viewers interpret THP as a social-media agency.
* Comments focus only on algorithm tactics.
* High views produce no qualified applications.
* The videos attract established scaling businesses rather than Level 1 microbusinesses.
* Founders agree emotionally but refuse evidence-based examination.

---

# 17. Review Rhythm

## After every video

Record:

* What the target viewer recognized.
* What they misunderstood.
* What language they used.
* Whether the CTA produced the intended action.

## After every three videos

Review:

* Audience fit.
* Category balance.
* Repeated objections.
* Application quality.
* Titles and framing.
* Whether the content is giving away interventions rather than building interpretation.

## After all 12 videos

Decide:

* Which series should continue.
* Which beliefs require deeper explanation.
* Which formats attract the right owners.
* Whether the readiness application is understandable.
* Whether the first-onboarder definition needs revision.
* Whether the service is being mistaken for an agency.
* Which viewer questions should become diagnostic inputs.
* Whether THP is ready to accept the first entity.

---

# 18. Content Boundaries

THP should not publish:

* Client details without permission.
* Financial claims without context.
* Diagnostic conclusions about businesses that were not examined.
* Generic prescriptive advice presented as universal truth.
* Tipper product promises unsupported by field evidence.
* "How to grow quickly" material that contradicts the stability doctrine.
* Content designed only because it is trending.
* Public criticism that humiliates struggling business owners.
* Fake certainty.

The content should be strong enough to challenge the owner without turning the owner into the problem.

---

# 19. Media-System Completion Condition

The YouTube architecture is ready to move into production when:

* The channel thesis is approved.
* The first 12-video sequence is approved.
* The visual system is defined.
* The public THP explanation exists.
* The application route exists.
* Each video has one learning objective and one CTA.
* The content record exists.
* The first three scripts are completed.
* The production workflow can release one principal video without building an oversized media operation.

The next operational step is:

> Write and produce Videos 1–3 while completing the public service explanation and application route.
