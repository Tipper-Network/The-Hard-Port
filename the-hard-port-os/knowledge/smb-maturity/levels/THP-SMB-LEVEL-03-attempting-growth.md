# Level 3 — Attempting Growth, Growth Model Not Yet Stable

> **Source of truth:** [`READ_FROM.md`](../../../READ_FROM.md) — § Level 3  
> **Parent model:** [`THP-SMB-LEVELS-001-maturity-model.md`](../THP-SMB-LEVELS-001-maturity-model.md)  
> **Prior level:** [`THP-SMB-LEVEL-02-stable-preparing-for-growth.md`](./THP-SMB-LEVEL-02-stable-preparing-for-growth.md)  
> **Not canonical** — boundary definition only; full maturity subsystem review pending.  
> **Founder direction:** confirmed 2026-07-21 (`founder_decisions: confirmed`). Defines the stage immediately following the first intervention; not the active operational scope.

## Formal definition

A Level 3 business has established the Level 2 operating foundation and is actively conducting deliberate experiments intended to increase demand, customers, participation, revenue, capacity, partnerships, or market reach.

Its core business remains viable, but its growth mechanism is not yet proven to be repeatable, financially responsible, operationally supportable, or compatible with the business’s identity and community.

Level 3 is not unstable in the same way as Level 1.

At Level 1, the business itself is unstable.

At Level 3, the business has a stable core, but the system intended to produce growth remains uncertain.

## Central Level 3 question

> Can the business produce growth repeatedly without destabilizing the value, relationships, operations, and identity responsible for its existing stability?

## Position within the maturity model

- Level 1 proves that the business exists.
- Level 2 proves that the business has a viable operating foundation.
- Level 3 tests whether that foundation can support deliberate growth.
- Level 4 begins when healthy growth becomes repeatable and produces meaningful recognition — provisional; see [Levels 4–7 (provisional)](../THP-SMB-LEVELS-001-maturity-model.md#levels-47-provisional).

The transition is:

> Stable core → Controlled growth experimentation → Repeatable recognized growth

## Level 3 Entry Gate

A business enters Level 3 when all of the following are true.

## The Level 2 foundation remains active

The business continues to meet the mandatory Level 2 conditions:

- Core offer clarity.
- Core customer clarity.
- Basic financial visibility.
- Repeatable core delivery.
- Identifiable retention or community relationships.
- Organized feedback and evidence.
- Reduced survival pressure.
- Some knowledge and routines externalized beyond the founder.

A business cannot enter Level 3 by abandoning stability in pursuit of growth.

## A specific growth objective has been selected

The business has defined what it is attempting to increase.

Examples include:

- Qualified customer acquisition.
- Repeat purchasing.
- Customer retention.
- Referrals.
- Community participation.
- Production capacity.
- Service capacity.
- Partnership distribution.
- Geographic reach.
- Access to a new customer segment.
- Revenue from a proven offer.
- Adoption of an additional channel.

“Get bigger” is not a valid growth objective.

## A growth constraint has been identified

The business has identified the current factor preventing progress.

Examples include:

- Insufficient qualified awareness.
- Weak conversion.
- Low retention.
- Limited capacity.
- Founder dependency.
- Poor distribution.
- Weak trust.
- Unclear pricing.
- Inconsistent communication.
- Limited community participation.
- Operational bottlenecks.

## A deliberate experiment is active

The experiment has moved beyond preparation.

Resources, time, money, people, or operational capacity are actively being committed to testing the growth hypothesis.

## The viable core is protected

The business has defined:

- Core customer.
- Core offer.
- Minimum quality standard.
- Financial survival threshold.
- Operational capacity boundary.
- Important customer relationship.
- Identity elements that must not be sacrificed.
- Conditions requiring the experiment to stop.

## Baseline evidence exists

The business understands its normal performance well enough to compare conditions before, during, and after the experiment.

## What qualifies as a growth experiment

Use [`THP-SMB-INSTRUMENT-001`](../instruments/THP-SMB-INSTRUMENT-001-growth-experiment-record.md) to record each experiment.

A growth activity qualifies as a deliberate Level 3 experiment only when it contains the following elements.

### Growth objective

What specific business condition should increase or improve?

### Constraint

What currently prevents that improvement?

### Hypothesis

What does the business believe will change the constraint?

The hypothesis should follow this structure:

> If we take this action for this customer or context, then this result should change because of this reason.

### Defined intervention

What will the business actually do differently?

### Target population or context

Which customers, audience, location, channel, offer, or community will experience the test?

### Baseline

What was happening before the experiment began?

### Boundaries

The experiment must define:

- Budget.
- Time or cycle limit.
- Team capacity.
- Operational capacity.
- Maximum acceptable disruption.
- Minimum customer-experience standard.

### Evidence requirements

What would indicate:

- Success.
- Partial success.
- Failure.
- Harm to the core business.
- Need for another test.

### Review decision

At the review point, the business must choose to:

- Continue.
- Adjust.
- Repeat.
- Expand.
- Pause.
- Abandon.
- Investigate further.

An activity is not a valid growth experiment merely because money was spent or activity increased.

## Observable Level 3 Conditions

## A. Demand

The business is actively testing whether demand can be increased or reached more reliably.

Observable conditions include:

- New acquisition channels are being tested.
- Existing channels are being intentionally improved.
- The business is testing whether its core offer performs with more people.
- Adjacent offers or customer contexts may be explored.
- Demand may rise unevenly during experiments.
- The business distinguishes acquired attention from qualified demand.
- Different customer cohorts are being compared.
- The business is beginning to understand acquisition cost and demand quality.
- Some growth attempts produce promising results while others fail.

The business is no longer asking only:

> “Do people want this?”

It is now asking:

> “Can we repeatedly reach more of the right people under responsible conditions?”

## B. Customer Understanding

The core customer remains defined, but the business is testing the limits of that definition.

Observable conditions include:

- The business tests whether the core customer can be reached through additional channels.
- Adjacent customer groups may be explored.
- Differences between customer cohorts become visible.
- The business compares acquisition behavior, purchasing behavior, retention, and feedback.
- New customers may value the business for different reasons.
- The owner is learning which customer differences matter operationally.
- The business avoids immediately redefining itself around every new response.
- Core customers and experimental customers are tracked separately.

Level 3 customer discovery is controlled expansion, not a return to broad guessing.

## C. Retention and Community

Growth introduces new people into an existing relationship system.

Observable conditions include:

- The business measures whether newly acquired customers return.
- Referral patterns are tested deliberately.
- Community participation may increase.
- New members may not share the same expectations as the original core.
- The business observes whether growth strengthens or weakens community cohesion.
- Feedback is compared between established and newly acquired customers.
- The business tests methods for welcoming, educating, or integrating new participants.
- Core supporters may take on informal advocacy or leadership roles.
- The business begins distinguishing community growth from audience growth.

The Level 3 community challenge is:

> Can new people enter without dissolving the relationships and meaning that made the community valuable?

## D. Financial Stability

The core financial foundation remains protected while growth economics are tested.

Observable conditions include:

- Growth spending has a defined budget.
- The business begins measuring acquisition costs.
- Revenue generated by different channels or cohorts is compared.
- Margins may change as volume increases.
- New operational costs become visible.
- The business tests whether increased demand produces increased contribution or merely increased workload.
- Cash-flow timing becomes more important.
- The business monitors whether growth activity is consuming the resources needed for core operations.
- Financial results remain uncertain but interpretable.

A Level 3 business should begin answering:

> “What does it cost us to create and support this growth?”

## E. Operations

Growth experiments expose the limits of the Level 2 operating foundation.

Observable conditions include:

- Capacity constraints become measurable.
- Increased demand reveals bottlenecks.
- Delivery quality may come under pressure.
- Workflows are adjusted based on actual volume.
- Responsibilities become more specialized.
- The business learns which tasks should be standardized.
- Customer support requirements may increase.
- Inventory, scheduling, production, fulfilment, or communication systems are tested under pressure.
- Operational changes are introduced without rebuilding the whole business at once.
- The business records where growth creates delays, errors, inconsistency, or founder intervention.

Level 3 does not require perfect operations.

It requires the ability to discover and address operational limits without losing control of the business.

## F. Founder Dependency

Growth places pressure on the founder’s role.

Observable conditions include:

- The founder can no longer personally supervise every new customer or activity.
- Decision rights must become clearer.
- More work is delegated.
- The founder may struggle to release control.
- Team members require clearer context and standards.
- The founder begins shifting from direct execution toward experiment ownership, interpretation, and system design.
- Customer trust must increasingly attach to the business rather than only the founder.
- Some decisions still return unnecessarily to the founder.
- Growth may reveal that founder dependency is the primary constraint.

The Level 3 founder question becomes:

> “Which parts of my involvement create value, and which parts prevent the business from growing?”

## G. Evidence and Decision-Making

Level 3 requires a formal experiment-learning rhythm.

Observable conditions include:

- Growth experiments are recorded.
- Baselines and target metrics are documented.
- Results are reviewed at defined points.
- Customer cohorts or channels are compared.
- Qualitative feedback is interpreted alongside numerical performance.
- The business distinguishes growth from seasonal, viral, or external effects.
- Failed experiments produce reusable knowledge.
- Decisions include a stated reason.
- Multiple variables are not changed unnecessarily at the same time.
- Learning is connected across experiments.
- The business can explain what it knows, what it suspects, and what remains unknown.

At Level 3, evidence becomes operational infrastructure.

## H. Identity and Market Position

The business is testing whether its identity remains coherent as reach expands.

Observable conditions include:

- More people encounter the brand.
- Communication is tested across channels or customer groups.
- The business learns which messages travel without distortion.
- New demand may pressure the business to broaden its identity.
- The business protects the value and principles central to its core position.
- Customers increasingly use recognizable language when describing the business.
- The business begins becoming known for a specific promise, experience, product, value, or role.
- The founder distinguishes adaptation from identity dilution.
- Recognition is emerging but is not yet consistently established.

Level 3 asks:

> “Can more people understand us without forcing us to become generic?”

## The Level 3 Founder Experience

The Level 3 founder often experiences the most misleading period of business development.

Visible activity increases.

More customers, opportunities, meetings, content, requests, partnerships, and operational demands may appear.

This can feel like success even when the underlying growth mechanism is weak.

The founder may think:

- “Something is finally moving.”
- “We need to take advantage of this momentum.”
- “I cannot tell whether this channel is actually working.”
- “We are selling more, but everything is becoming harder.”
- “Our old systems are starting to break.”
- “New customers do not behave like our original customers.”
- “I am becoming the bottleneck again.”
- “We need to hire, but I do not know which role comes first.”
- “I do not want to slow down and lose the opportunity.”
- “I also do not want growth to destroy what we built.”

The emotional transition is:

> From protecting stability
> to interpreting pressure.

The founder must learn that not every increase is healthy growth.

## Primary Level 3 Risks

## Risk 1 — Confusing activity with growth

More posts, customers, meetings, orders, employees, and revenue do not automatically mean that the business has developed a growth mechanism.

The business may simply be busier.

## Risk 2 — Destabilizing the viable core

Growth activity may consume the money, attention, people, or operating capacity required to serve existing customers.

## Risk 3 — Acquiring the wrong customers

A channel may produce volume while attracting customers with weak fit, poor retention, high support needs, or low margins.

## Risk 4 — Scaling founder dependency

Hiring people without changing decision structures can create more people waiting for the founder.

## Risk 5 — Identity dilution

The business may weaken its position to appeal to a broader audience.

## Risk 6 — Operational collapse

Growth may exceed fulfilment, service, production, inventory, or support capacity.

## Risk 7 — False attribution

A business may attribute growth to its experiment when the result came from seasonality, a trend, one influential customer, or an external event.

## Risk 8 — Experiment accumulation

The business may begin several experiments simultaneously and lose the ability to interpret any of them.

## Risk 9 — Permanent experimentation

The business may repeatedly test new tactics without consolidating what has been learned into a growth mechanism.

---

## The Level 3 Failure Loop

A common Level 3 failure loop is:

1. The Level 2 business experiences early momentum.
2. The owner launches several growth actions.
3. Activity and demand increase.
4. Capacity, quality, and customer relationships come under pressure.
5. The founder becomes central again.
6. Evidence becomes fragmented.
7. The business cannot identify which actions created value.
8. Costs and workload rise faster than retained value.
9. Existing customers receive a weaker experience.
10. The business either returns to survival mode or continues expanding an unhealthy system.

THP must interrupt this loop before temporary growth becomes structural damage.

## Level 3 Operating Doctrine

## Protect the Level 2 foundation

Growth experiments must not consume the business’s survival resources or destroy its viable core.

## One primary constraint at a time

The business may have several problems, but each experiment should address one primary constraint.

## Track acquired cohorts separately

Customers gained through different channels, campaigns, partnerships, offers, or locations should not be treated as identical.

## Measure retained value

Growth should be judged by what remains after acquisition:

- Retained customers.
- Repeat purchases.
- Contribution margin.
- Community participation.
- Referrals.
- Operational learning.
- Brand recognition.
- Improved capacity.

## Treat operational pressure as evidence

Delays, mistakes, complaints, founder overload, and quality changes are not merely inconveniences.

They reveal the limits of the current system.

## Consolidate before expanding

When an experiment works, the business should document and stabilize the mechanism before adding another major growth initiative.

## Growth-experiment states

Every formal growth experiment should move through defined states.

## Proposed

A growth hypothesis has been identified but not approved.

## Designed

The objective, hypothesis, boundaries, evidence, and review point are defined.

## Active

The experiment is currently being executed.

## Under Review

The test period or cycle has ended and evidence is being interpreted.

## Invalid

The experiment cannot produce a trustworthy conclusion because execution, tracking, or external conditions made the result unreliable.

## Failed

The hypothesis was not supported or the experiment created unacceptable harm.

## Inconclusive

The evidence is insufficient or conflicting.

## Promising

The experiment produced useful positive evidence but requires repetition.

## Validated

The result has been repeated sufficiently under comparable conditions without violating the protected-core thresholds.

## Operationalized

The validated mechanism has been integrated into normal business operations.

A Level 3 business should not describe a single promising experiment as a proven growth system.

---

## Level 3 Regression Conditions

A Level 3 business may regress to Level 2 when:

- The active growth experiment ends or is paused.
- The Level 2 foundation remains intact.
- The business returns to preparation, analysis, or redesign.
- No active deliberate growth experiment remains.

This is not necessarily failure.

It may represent responsible consolidation.

A Level 3 business regresses to Level 1 when:

- Growth activity destroys financial or operational stability.
- The viable core is no longer functioning.
- Immediate survival again controls decision-making.
- The business can no longer meet the Level 2 gate.

Temporary pressure does not automatically cause regression.

Regression occurs when the underlying gate is no longer sustained.

## The Level 3 Exit Gate

A business exits Level 3 and enters Level 4 when it has demonstrated at least one repeatable, healthy growth mechanism and that mechanism is producing meaningful recognition within the relevant customer or community context.

The following conditions must be demonstrated.

## A validated growth mechanism

The business can identify a specific mechanism connecting:

- A target customer or community.
- A channel, partnership, offer, or growth intervention.
- A repeatable acquisition or participation behavior.
- A viable conversion process.
- Acceptable delivery.
- Retained value.

## Repetition

The mechanism has produced comparable positive results across at least three complete growth cycles or customer cohorts.

A single successful campaign does not qualify.

## Core stability preserved

During the growth cycles:

- Core quality remained within the defined standard.
- Financial survival was not threatened.
- Existing customer relationships were not materially damaged.
- The business identity remained coherent.
- The founder did not become the only mechanism holding the growth together.

## Interpretable growth economics

The business understands, with reasonable accuracy:

- What the growth mechanism costs.
- What value it creates.
- How long value takes to appear.
- Which customers or cohorts are worth retaining.
- Which operational costs increase with growth.
- Whether the mechanism produces contribution rather than merely revenue.

## Operational support

The business can support the mechanism through repeatable workflows, defined responsibilities, and known capacity boundaries.

## Retention or community health

New customers or participants demonstrate sufficient retention, repeat behavior, referral, participation, or relationship value.

## Emerging recognition

The relevant market or community is beginning to recognize the business for a consistent value or identity.

Recognition does not yet need to be broad.

It must be observable and connected to the growth mechanism.

## Institutional learning

The business can explain:

- Why the mechanism works.
- Where it works.
- For whom it works.
- What it costs.
- What could cause it to fail.
- What must be protected as it continues.

---

## Proof That Level 3 Has Been Completed

## Demand evidence

- Repeated qualified acquisition.
- Consistent conversion behavior.
- Comparable performance across multiple cycles or cohorts.
- Reduced dependence on isolated spikes.
- Clear connection between intervention and demand outcome.

## Customer evidence

- Defined customer cohorts.
- Documented acquisition motivations.
- Evidence of fit among newly acquired customers.
- Retention, repeat, or referral behavior.
- Identification of poor-fit acquisition sources.

## Community evidence

- New participants integrate without destroying core cohesion.
- Community participation remains stable or improves.
- Feedback from new and established members is interpretable.
- Core supporters remain active.
- Shared identity or value becomes more visible.

## Financial evidence

- Growth costs are recorded.
- Revenue and contribution are distinguished.
- Core margins remain visible.
- Cash-flow pressure is manageable.
- Growth does not depend on uncontrolled subsidies.
- The business can explain the economics of the mechanism.

## Operational evidence

- Increased demand is supported without recurring emergency failure.
- Capacity thresholds are known.
- Workflows have been adapted.
- Responsibilities are clear.
- Quality remains within defined limits.
- Founder intervention is not required for every transaction.

## Founder evidence

- The founder can interpret growth rather than merely chase it.
- Decision rights are becoming distributed.
- The founder knows where personal involvement remains necessary.
- Growth does not depend entirely on founder energy or personal relationships.
- Strategic review continues during periods of higher activity.

## Learning evidence

- Experiment records exist.
- Invalid and failed experiments are preserved.
- Promising results were repeated before validation.
- Decisions are traceable to evidence.
- The business can distinguish correlation from a credible causal explanation.
- The validated mechanism has been documented.

## Identity and recognition evidence

- Customer descriptions are becoming consistent.
- The business is increasingly associated with a clear value or position.
- Recognition appears within the relevant community or market.
- The identity survives movement across the tested channel or context.
- Broader reach has not made the business generic.

---

## What Level 3 Is Not

A business is not automatically Level 3 because:

- Revenue increased.
- It hired a marketing agency.
- It began posting more frequently.
- It launched paid advertisements.
- It opened another social-media account.
- It received investment.
- It hired employees.
- It developed a new product.
- It entered a partnership.
- It received one large order.
- It went viral.
- Its founder became more visible.
- It is overwhelmed by increased work.

Level 3 requires a deliberate active growth experiment built upon a functioning Level 2 foundation.

## THP's role at Level 3

At Level 3, THP helps the business convert growth activity into institutional learning.

## Protect the viable core

THP identifies:

- What must remain stable.
- Which customers must be protected.
- Which quality standards cannot fall.
- Which financial thresholds cannot be crossed.
- Which identity elements must remain coherent.

## Design the experiment system

THP helps establish:

- Growth objectives.
- Constraint definitions.
- Hypotheses.
- Experiment boundaries.
- Baselines.
- Success and failure criteria.
- Review points.
- Decision records.

## Interpret growth quality

THP helps distinguish:

- Attention from qualified demand.
- Revenue from contribution.
- Acquisition from retention.
- Audience growth from community growth.
- Temporary momentum from repeatable mechanisms.
- Operational pressure from genuine capacity failure.
- New opportunities from identity dilution.

## Structure customer and community evidence

THP organizes:

- Cohort feedback.
- Repeat behavior.
- Referral behavior.
- Community participation.
- Reasons for joining.
- Reasons for disengaging.
- Differences between core and newly acquired customers.

## Support operational consolidation

THP helps the business turn successful experiments into:

- Repeatable workflows.
- Defined responsibilities.
- Capacity rules.
- Customer-experience standards.
- Communication systems.
- Decision protocols.

## Establish the Tipper research layer

Where appropriate, Tipper supports:

- Structured customer and community feedback.
- Participation records.
- Relationship visibility.
- Cohort comparison.
- Community questions.
- Suggestions and feedback tickets.
- Evidence of repeat involvement.
- Learning across experiments.

Tipper should not be introduced merely because the business is growing.

It should be introduced where it makes the relationships and evidence behind growth more visible.
