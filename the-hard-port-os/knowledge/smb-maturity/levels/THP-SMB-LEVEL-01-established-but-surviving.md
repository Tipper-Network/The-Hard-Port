# Level 1 — Established but Surviving

> **Source of truth:** [`READ_FROM.md`](../../../READ_FROM.md) — § Level 1  
> **Parent model:** [`THP-SMB-LEVELS-001-maturity-model.md`](../THP-SMB-LEVELS-001-maturity-model.md)  
> **Entry gate:** **Confirmed** 2026-07-21 — see [`Founder Decisions Confirmed`](../THP-SMB-LEVELS-001-maturity-model.md#confirmed-level-1-entry-gate)  
> **Not canonical** (full seven-level system) until maturity subsystem formally reviewed.

## Formal definition

A Level 1 business has demonstrated that real customers will pay for and receive its offer, but it has not yet established the demand, retention, financial, operational, or learning systems needed for stability.

The business exists. Its continued existence is not yet dependable.

## Entry gate

**Status:** `confirmed` (2026-07-21)

A business enters Level 1 when:

1. It has a defined offer.
2. Multiple unrelated paying customers have purchased the offer.
3. The offer has been delivered more than once.
4. The business remains actively operating.
5. The founder intends to continue building it.

No universal transaction threshold is used beyond the requirement for multiple unrelated customers and repeated delivery.

The business must have enough completed customer cycles to produce meaningful evidence for diagnosis.

At qualification, application claims for these criteria require **verification** — see [`THP-SMB-INSTRUMENT-002` § Application Inputs](../instruments/THP-SMB-INSTRUMENT-002-first-onboarder-diagnostic.md#application-inputs).

### Excluded (not Level 1 — first onboarder)

- Pure ideas
- Pre-launch projects
- Hobby pages without transactions
- One-off freelance accidents
- Businesses that have stopped operating
- Audiences that have not developed an offer

These may eventually require a Level 0 definition. They are not currently THP's first onboarder.

> A single sale proves demand may exist. It does not prove an operating business exists.

## Observable Level 1 conditions

A business does not need every symptom, but should display **several across mandatory dimensions**.

Assess using dimensions A–H from the [parent model](../THP-SMB-LEVELS-001-maturity-model.md#eight-assessment-dimensions).

### A — Demand

- Sales occur irregularly.
- The owner cannot predict where the next customer will come from.
- Demand may depend on discounts, personal contacts, trends, or temporary visibility.
- The owner does not know which offer has the strongest underlying demand.
- Attention does not reliably convert into purchases.

### B — Customer understanding

- The owner describes the customer too broadly.
- Customer assumptions are based on impressions rather than evidence.
- The owner knows who interacts with content but not necessarily who buys.
- The business cannot clearly explain why customers choose it.
- The owner treats different customer groups as though they want the same thing.

### C — Retention and community

- Some customers return, but the reasons are unclear.
- Referrals happen informally.
- The business has followers but cannot identify its core supporters.
- Feedback is fragmented across comments, messages, and conversations.
- There is no consistent way for customers to participate.
- Customers are treated as separate transactions.

### D — Financial stability

- Revenue fluctuates significantly.
- The founder may use personal money to keep the business operating.
- The business focuses on sales volume without understanding margin.
- One client, customer, campaign, or season may determine survival.
- Financial decisions are reactive.
- The owner cannot confidently forecast the following month.

### E — Operations

- Work is managed through memory, messages, or improvised routines.
- Quality changes depending on workload.
- Delivery becomes difficult when demand increases.
- The owner handles most failures personally.
- There is little documentation.
- Content production may be more organized than the actual business operation.

### F — Founder dependency

- The founder is the salesperson, operator, customer support representative, and strategist.
- Important relationships exist primarily through the founder.
- Decisions stop when the founder is unavailable.
- The brand and founder identity may be inseparable.
- Delegation often creates more work because systems do not exist.
- The founder is tired but afraid to release control.

### G — Evidence and decision-making

- The business tries many tactics without recording what happened.
- Posting, trends, boosts, rebranding, and promotions are disconnected experiments.
- Success is measured through visible engagement rather than business effects.
- The owner changes direction before enough evidence is collected.
- Customer feedback is heard but not systematically interpreted.
- Decisions are based on urgency, imitation, or anxiety.

### H — Identity and market position

- The business may look visually branded but lack a clear position.
- Messaging changes frequently.
- The owner explains the business through products rather than value.
- The business imitates competitors or trends.
- Customers may know what it sells but not what makes it distinct.
- The business has not yet developed a stable role within a community.

## The Level 1 founder experience

The founder usually experiences several contradictions.

They may think:

- "I know people want this because some people buy it."

But also:

- "I do not know why more people are not buying."

They may believe:

- The algorithm is suppressing them.
- They need more content.
- They need to post consistently.
- Their branding is not professional enough.
- Their price is too high.
- Their market does not understand them.
- Their business model may be fundamentally broken.
- They need to hire someone for marketing.
- One successful campaign will solve the problem.

Their deeper experience is:

- Constant urgency.
- Decision fatigue.
- Difficulty separating symptoms from causes.
- Fear that slowing down will kill the business.
- Fear that continuing the same way will also kill it.
- Personal identification with every business result.
- Increasing doubt about their own judgment.

## Primary Level 1 risk

The main risk is not merely business closure.

The deeper risk is:

> The founder repeatedly changes the visible parts of the business before understanding the system producing the results.

### The reactive loop

```text
Sales become weak
        ↓
Owner assumes visibility is the problem
        ↓
More content is produced
        ↓
Attention increases temporarily
        ↓
Sales remain inconsistent
        ↓
Owner changes branding, pricing, or offers
        ↓
Existing customer evidence becomes harder to interpret
        ↓
Founder becomes more doubtful and reactive
        ↓
Cycle repeats
```

**This is where THP should first intervene.**

## Exit gate (Level 1 → Level 2)

A business exits Level 1 only when it has created enough stability to make **deliberate decisions**.

### Demand clarity

- Identified at least one core offer with recurring evidence of demand.
- Knows which customer group is most valuable at this stage.
- Sales are not entirely dependent on one temporary campaign or personal relationship.

### Customer clarity

- Can describe who buys, why they buy, and what causes them to return.
- Customer understanding is based on observed behavior and direct feedback.
- Has separated core customer from broader audience.

### Retention or community evidence

- Recognizable core of customers returns, refers, participates, or remains connected.
- Repeatable way to gather feedback.
- Can identify early community patterns — not treating all customers as isolated transactions.

### Financial visibility

- Owner understands basic revenue, costs, and margins.
- Business can meet immediate obligations with less chaos.
- Can make a basic short-term forecast.
- Survival is no longer determined entirely week by week.

Does not necessarily mean high profitability — means the financial condition is **visible and manageable** for intentional decisions.

### Operational consistency

- Core offer delivered with reasonably consistent quality.
- Essential workflows are repeatable.
- Business knows current capacity and major operational failure points.
- Increased demand no longer produces complete disorder.

### Reduced founder reactivity

- Founder can step back long enough to evaluate the business.
- Not every decision requires immediate founder intervention.
- At least some responsibilities, routines, or information externalized.
- Founder has begun separating personal identity from business performance.

### Evidence system

- Records important customer, sales, content, and operational evidence.
- Experiments have a stated purpose.
- Results influence later decisions.
- Owner can explain why they are making the next change.

### Identity clarity

- Business can clearly state what it provides, for whom, and why it matters.
- Communication is becoming consistent.
- Identity based on observed value, not only founder aspiration.
- Customers can begin recognizing what the business stands for.

## Proof that Level 1 has been completed

THP must not declare the transition based only on the founder feeling better.

Evidence should include a **combination** of:

### Behavioral evidence

- Repeat customers.
- Referrals.
- Community participation.
- Customer responses.
- Consistent purchasing patterns.

### Commercial evidence

- Reduced revenue volatility.
- Improved offer performance.
- Better margin understanding.
- Reduced dependence on discounts.
- More reliable short-term forecasting.

### Operational evidence

- Repeated delivery quality.
- Fewer emergency failures.
- Basic documented workflows.
- Clear capacity understanding.

### Decision evidence

- Recorded experiments.
- Decisions connected to evidence.
- Fewer random strategic changes.
- Clear explanations for what the business will test next.

### Founder evidence

- Reduced daily reactivity.
- Improved delegation.
- Greater clarity about business priorities.
- Ability to distinguish the business's condition from the founder's personal worth.
