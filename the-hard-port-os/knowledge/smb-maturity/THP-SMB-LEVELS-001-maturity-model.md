# SMB Maturity Model

> **Status:** `candidate`, `non_canonical` — **not Canon** until the founder confirms classification rules and all seven level gates have been reviewed.  
> **First onboarder:** [`THP-SMB-001`](../../docs/04-market/first-onboarder/THP-SMB-001-FIRST-ONBOARDER.md)  
> **Transition form:** [`THP-SMB-002`](../../active/forms/THP-SMB-002-level-1-to-level-2-transition.md)

## Core principle

> A single sale proves demand may exist. It does not prove an operating business exists.

## Classification logic

### Primary rule

A business's **overall institutional level** is the **highest stage whose required gate it has fully completed**.

The level is **not** based on:

- appearance;
- revenue alone;
- followers;
- or what the founder calls the business.

### Weakest gate controls progression

A business **cannot advance** merely because one area is strong.

| Misleading signal | Does not prove |
|---|---|
| Strong sales with chaotic delivery | Stability |
| Many followers without repeat customers | Community |
| A viral campaign | Growth |
| Hiring employees | Operational maturity |
| Revenue growth | Scalability |
| Brand recognition | Viable business model |

### Dimension-specific maturity

A business may display different maturity **by dimension**. Example:

> Currently **Level 2** overall, with **Level 4** brand recognition and **Level 1** operational weaknesses.

THP records both:

- **Overall institutional level** — controlled by the weakest mandatory gate
- **Per-dimension maturity** — diagnostic detail across the eight dimensions below

This produces a more accurate diagnostic than assigning one vague score.

## Eight assessment dimensions

Every business is assessed across the same eight dimensions at every level. What changes is the **degree of maturity required** to pass each gate.

| ID | Dimension | Core question |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Demand** | Do people genuinely want what the business offers? |
| **B** | **Customer understanding** | Does the business understand who buys and why? |
| **C** | **Retention and community** | Do people return, participate, recommend, or remain connected? |
| **D** | **Financial stability** | Can the business continue operating? |
| **E** | **Operations** | Can the business deliver its value consistently? |
| **F** | **Founder dependency** | Can the business function without the founder controlling everything? |
| **G** | **Evidence and decision-making** | Does the business learn from its actions? |
| **H** | **Identity and market position** | Does the business have a recognizable and meaningful position? |

### Dimension examination criteria

**A — Demand:** Real purchases; purchase frequency; customer urgency; offer relevance; dependence on discounts; whether demand exists without constant persuasion.

**B — Customer understanding:** Main customer groups; motivations; problems or desires served; reasons customers choose or leave; differences between followers and buyers.

**C — Retention and community:** Repeat purchases; referrals; participation; customer relationships; feedback; identifiable core supporters; community cohesion.

**D — Financial stability:** Revenue consistency; cash-flow pressure; margins; cost awareness; ability to meet obligations; dependence on one customer or campaign; founder subsidization.

**E — Operations:** Fulfilment; quality consistency; workflows; capacity; documentation; team responsibilities; failure points.

**F — Founder dependency:** Decision concentration; customer relationships tied to founder; founder-led sales and delivery; delegation; burnout; identity separation between founder and business.

**G — Evidence and decision-making:** Data collection; customer feedback; experiment tracking; decision records; distinguishing causes from symptoms; whether prior actions inform later decisions.

**H — Identity and market position:** Value proposition; business identity; reputation; brand recognition; message consistency; relevance within a community; what the business is known for.

## Level 1 — Established but Surviving

**ID:** `level_01_established_but_surviving`

### Formal definition

A Level 1 business has demonstrated that real customers will pay for and receive its offer, but it has not yet established the demand, retention, financial, operational, or learning systems needed for stability.

The business **exists**. Its continued existence is **not yet dependable**.

### Entry gate (summary)

A business enters Level 1 when **all** of the following are true:

- It has a defined offer.
- Multiple unrelated customers have paid for that offer.
- The offer has been delivered more than once.
- The business is still actively operating.
- The founder intends to continue operating it as a business.

**Excludes:** pure ideas; pre-launch projects; hobby pages without transactions; one-off freelance accidents; stopped businesses; audiences without an offer. (Level 0 may be defined later; not the first onboarder.)

### Exit gate (summary)

A business exits Level 1 when it demonstrates stability sufficient for deliberate decisions across:

- demand clarity;
- customer clarity;
- retention or community evidence;
- financial visibility;
- operational consistency;
- reduced founder reactivity;
- evidence system;
- identity clarity.

Full exit criteria, observable conditions, founder experience, primary risk, and proof requirements: [`THP-SMB-LEVEL-01-established-but-surviving.md`](./levels/THP-SMB-LEVEL-01-established-but-surviving.md).

## Founder Decisions Pending

Working defaults — **require founder confirmation** before this model becomes operational authority:

1. **Level 1 entry:** A business becomes Level 1 after serving multiple unrelated paying customers and repeatedly delivering its offer.
2. **Weakest gate rule:** A business's overall level is controlled by the **lowest mandatory gate**, not its strongest visible achievement.
3. **Level 2 financial bar:** Level 2 does not require large profits, but it **does** require financial visibility and reduced survival pressure.
4. **Community threshold:** Community begins with **identifiable repeat relationships**, not a large audience.
5. **Founder dependency at Level 2:** Founder dependency may still exist at Level 2, but the business must have begun **externalizing knowledge and routines**.
6. **Spike exclusion:** A temporary spike **cannot** be used as evidence of progression.
7. **Stability period:** Stability must persist across a meaningful operating period — provisionally **three months** or **three complete business cycles**.

**Open decision (founder):** Should stability be proven through a fixed period (e.g. three months) or through a number of completed business cycles that varies by business?

**THP recommendation:** Business cycles, with three months as a practical minimum when the business has frequent transactions.
