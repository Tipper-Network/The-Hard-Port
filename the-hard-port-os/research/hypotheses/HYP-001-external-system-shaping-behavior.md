# HYP-001 — External Systems Shape Behavior Without Conscious Self-Authorship

## Hypothesis

Within a repeated small-business environment, when a person does not deliberately examine and author their role, their behavior becomes increasingly shaped by external systems, identities, incentives, and expectations.

## Context

- Small-business owners and workers
- Repeated participation in roles and routines
- Environments with visible incentives, expectations, and decision structures

## Proposed Mechanism

Repeated behavior produces familiarity and social reinforcement. Familiar patterns become default expectations. Those expectations reduce the likelihood that a person questions the role or system producing them.

## Expected Observations

If the hypothesis is useful, we may observe:

- People repeating role behavior after its original purpose disappears.
- Owners retaining decisions because “that is what the owner does.”
- Workers avoiding permitted decisions because the surrounding expectations discourage them.
- Behavior changing after decision rights, incentives, or role expectations change.
- People describing structural behavior as fixed personal identity.

## Evidence That Would Increase Confidence

- Repeated observations across independent businesses
- Behavior changes following a bounded structural intervention
- Stable differences between otherwise comparable decision environments
- Longitudinal evidence showing role expectations precede behavior change

## Evidence That Would Reduce Confidence

- People consistently resisting system pressure without deliberate reflection
- Behavior remaining unchanged after material structural and incentive changes
- Capability, information, resources, or risk explaining the observations better
- Identity explanations adding no predictive or diagnostic value
- Effects appearing only in one business or owner relationship

## Alternative Hypotheses

- People understand the system but rationally accept its constraints.
- Missing capability or information, not identification, produces the behavior.
- Resource scarcity prevents alternative action.
- Selection effects place people with matching preferences into the same systems.
- Power and consequence explain compliance without requiring an identity mechanism.

## Current Evidence

No structured evidence has been attached.

The Session 01 statement is the origin of the hypothesis, not supporting evidence.

## Next Actions

1. Define “conscious self-authorship” through observable criteria.
2. Identify a bounded decision behavior suitable for observation.
3. Collect supporting and contradicting cases.
4. Design an intervention that changes structure without coaching identity.
5. Compare the structural intervention against an explicit reflection intervention.
