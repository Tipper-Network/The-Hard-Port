# Research Program 001 — Systems, Identity, and Self-Authorship

## Research Object

Investigate how conscious beings construct, inhabit, become shaped by, and intentionally redesign the systems that affect their reality.

This research object is broader than the current canonical mission. The initial bounded laboratory remains small businesses.

## Primary Question

How does participation in a business system shape the behavior, identity, perception, and decisions of the people inside it?

## Initial Hypothesis

[`HYP-001 — External Systems Shape Behavior Without Conscious Self-Authorship`](./hypotheses/HYP-001-external-system-shaping-behavior.md)

## Research Boundary

### Included in Version 0.1

- Small-business owners and workers
- Repeated business routines
- Incentives and expectations
- Owner and role identity
- Decision dependency
- Observable behavior inside business systems

### Excluded Until Further Definition

- Universal claims about consciousness
- Clinical or therapeutic identity models
- Communities as a separate research laboratory
- Claims that observation automatically produces self-authorship
- Claims that technology can validate human identity

## Initial Evidence Plan

1. Interview owners about decisions or roles they struggle to release.
2. Observe whether behavior differs when authority, incentives, or expectations change.
3. Record cases where individuals resist system pressure despite repeated exposure.
4. Separate self-reported identity from observable decisions and behavior.
5. Compare owner-dependent and distributed-decision environments.
6. Test alternative explanations such as capability, information access, risk, and resource scarcity.
