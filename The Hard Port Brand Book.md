# The Hard Port — Brand Book

## One line
**We give a f*ck. We just don't care.**

We care about the work and the outcome. We do not care about the client's comfort, feelings, excuses or ego. Every word, layout and interaction gets checked against that split. If something makes the brand look like it wants to be liked, it is wrong.

## Who we are
The Hard Port (THP) is a no-bullshit media and strategy agency for small businesses that are "doing fine". Fine is the problem. We bring clarity, the client brings commitment.

"Hard a-port" is the command you shout to swing a ship hard before it hits the rocks. That is the whole company in one phrase.

Founder: Tarek H Ghosn, based in Lebanon. Site: thehardport.com. Instagram: @thehardport.

## The archetype
**The Uncompromising Navigator.** A strategic partner, not a helper.

Picture a veteran captain. She looks at a struggling studio owner or gym operator, points at the horizon and tells them the truth they have been avoiding. Calm, unshakeable and immune to excuses, drama and ego.

- **Voice:** pointed and hard. Level 2 to 3 on the aggression ladder. It never starts at level 4 and it never softens at the call to action.
- **What it does:** it punches the behaviour and hands the person a way out in the same breath. Example: "You are choosing to stay busy because being busy feels safer than fixing the system."
- **Posture:** we do not reassure. We demand clarity and put a system in place.
- **Philosophy:** we do not help. We do our job so yours gets easier.

## The model, for one year
One target market, one product or service, one conversion tool, one traffic source, one year. Details are in Promise, problems and desires.

## What we challenge
- The post-and-boost cycle. Chasing the algorithm makes noise, not a business.
- False competition. The only comparison that matters is you against you.
- Agencies, gurus and courses that sell reassurance.
- Motion mistaken for progress.

## What we do differently
- We change the question from "how do I get attention?" to "what is my business worth?".
- We build the business first (identity, offer, customers, community, operations). Content becomes a result of it, not the engine.
- The core promise: in 30 weeks, take a business from running on the owner to running on a system.
- We do not help. We do our job so yours gets easier.

## Who it is for
Small, community-driven service businesses: service businesses and event planners (digital), gyms, studios and event halls (physical). Same model from two ends. The owners are stagnant, comfortable and quietly aware something is off. They put their own money in. Their identity is fused with the business. They have likely been burned before.

**Not for** anyone who wants to be reassured, coddled or sold to gently. The copy is allowed to make some readers leave.

### What they fear
- Being seen to fail, by a spouse, an employee, a competitor.
- Admitting the business runs them, not the other way around.
- That years already spent are wasted if they change course.
- Being sold to, again.

### What moves them
- Relief, not hustle. The real dream outcome is "stop feeling like I'm failing quietly".
- Specificity over hype. Small, checkable claims beat big vague ones.
- Being told "you're not ready" often works better than "you qualify".

## The brand in five rules
1. Short sentences do the damage. Long ones explain.
2. Never announce honesty, edge or boldness. Be it.
3. Punch the behaviour, never the person's worth, and hand them a way out in the same breath.
4. The nautical world is the only metaphor world: sea, sail, port, rocks, anchor, drift, crew, ship.
5. Intensity rises as the reader goes deeper. Darker, tighter, bolder. Never softer near the call to action.

## The promise
Everything below hangs off this line.

**In 30 weeks, your business goes from running on you to running on a system.**

The programme is now 30 weeks. This is the working wording. Confirm it, or give us the line you want to be held to.

## Problems
Three problems. Each one is a name, the line the owner says to themselves, the line we say back, and the truth we stand on. The lines we say back are drafts. Edit them freely, but keep them this plain.

### 1. Money: the gap between profit and reality
- **The owner feels:** always in survival mode.
- **We say:** "You're not building. You're surviving."
- **The truth:** a small business is made to add and create, not just stay alive.

### 2. Manual effort: no systems, same problem every day
- **The owner feels:** same problems every day.
- **We say:** "Same problem. Different Tuesday."
- **The truth:** what nobody checks falls to its lowest standard. A system lets everyone in the business connect to the core of the work.

### 3. Friction, trust and chaos: the owner can't step away
- **The owner feels:** I can't step away.
- **We say:** "Leave for a week. See what breaks."
- **The truth:** feeling productive and being productive are not the same thing.

## Desires
Three things the owner wants. Each one is what they want, what we say, and the truth behind it.

### 1. Predictable money
- **The owner wants:** to know what is coming in.
- **We say:** "No more guessing."
- **The truth:** money stops being a surprise once the numbers are rechecked, received and used.

### 2. Control
- **The owner wants:** one word from them to change the course of the flow, and to feel their own effort seen and appreciated.
- **We say:** "Hand on the wheel."
- **The truth:** the owner should steer. Right now the business steers the owner.
- **Word note:** this replaces "leverage", which is on our banned list. "Control" is the plain word. "The wheel" keeps it nautical. We avoid "driver's seat" (a driving image, not a sailing one) and "power" (sounds like hustle talk).

### 3. A quiet head
- **The owner wants:** to respond, not react.
- **We say:** "Quiet head. Steady hand."
- **The truth:** when the work runs on a system, the owner stops firefighting and starts deciding.

## Solutions
**Open.** We have not worked out the solutions yet, and that is deliberate. We will learn them with the first testers of the Port. The five sprints (Structure, Costs, Profit, Clients, Team) are raw material, not the answer. Use the Solution model brief to build each one.

## How it reads on a page
1. **Problem first, hard.** Name the pain in their words. Level 2 or 3.
2. **Desire second, plain.** What life looks like with it fixed. Relief, not hype.
3. **Solution third, short.** The route and the promise. Never soft near the call to action.

Keep every line at the reading level of the owner's own words. If a line needs explaining, cut it.

# Solution model brief

A working brief for building the solutions. Nothing here is decided. It is the frame we fill in with the first testers of the Port.

## The rule
One problem, one change, one number the owner can check. If we cannot say it in a sentence, it is not a solution yet.

## Who tests it
The target market is the first testers. Small, community-driven service businesses from either end: digital (service businesses, event planners) or physical (gyms, studios, event halls). Owners who recognise at least one of the three problems and will commit.

## How testers are found
We are in the testing phase of the 30-week Port programme.

1. **Cold call.** We call small businesses and talk to the owner. We are not selling. We are listening.
2. **Fit conversation.** We find out whether the work we plan to do fits how their business is built.
3. **Test for little money.** If it fits, we work with them for little money. They get real work, we get to test the solution.
4. **Learn and adjust.** What happens with each tester changes the solution, the line and the next call.

### Fit conversation: draft questions
Use their own words. Keep them short.
- What happens in your business when you are away for a week?
- Which problem shows up every day, the same way?
- Do you know what money is coming in next month?
- Who else in the business can make the decision you make?
- If we fixed one thing in 30 weeks, which would it be?

A tester fits when they recognise at least one of the three problems, the owner makes most of the calls, and they will commit to the 30 weeks.

## The questions for each problem
Answer these for each problem, in the owner's words, in this order.

1. **Before.** What does the owner do today, and what does it cost them?
2. **The change.** What is different afterwards? One sentence.
3. **What we install.** The system, routine or tool that makes the change stick.
4. **Who does what.** What we do. What the owner does.
5. **Time.** How long until the owner feels it?
6. **The number.** The one figure the owner can check themselves.
7. **The desire it delivers.** Predictable money, control or a quiet head.
8. **What would make us stop or change it.** The sign it is not working.

## The board
Fill one row per problem as testers teach us.

| Problem | The change | What we install | The number | Time | Desire it delivers | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Survival mode (money gap) | | | | | Predictable money | Open |
| Same problems every day (no systems) | | | | | Control | Open |
| Can't step away (friction, chaos) | | | | | A quiet head | Open |

## How we learn
- One change at a time with each tester, so we know what worked.
- After each tester, note what they said in their own words. Their words become the next headline.
- Review the board with the numbers once a month.
- Anything that does not move a number gets cut.

## What feeds the brand
When a solution works, it becomes: a line in our voice, one proof the owner can check, and one more rung of the funnel. Nothing goes on the website until a tester has lived it.

# Voice

## The canonical letter
The emotional centre of the brand. Everything else is checked against it.

> The sea never cared for a sailor's feelings. Only whether he was prepared.
>
> When you set sail, the sea reminds you you're supposed to be going somewhere. When's the last time you noticed you'd stopped moving with a direction?
>
> Ask yourself the question: Am I where I want to be?
>
> We call you so you don't feel like you're asking for help, because you're not asking for help, you're asking for clarity. We won't help you. We'll just do our job, so yours gets easier.

## Posture
Calm, unshakeable, immune to excuses, drama and ego. A veteran captain, not a cheerleader and not a troll. We do not reassure. We demand clarity and put a system in place.

## Rules
- Second person, always "you".
- Short by default. One idea per line.
- Say it, do not describe it. A line that explains what the brand is, is not that thing.
- Tone escalates as the reader moves down the page or through the carousel. It never flattens and never softens at the call to action.
- Aggression is calibrated. Punch the behaviour, not the worth. Give the person a way out in the same breath. Think good coach, not troll.
- Specific beats big. A number you can check beats an adjective.

## Aggression ladder
| Level | Sounds like | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Dry | "Fine is the problem." | Openers, captions |
| 2. Pointed | "You stopped moving a while ago." | Body copy, carousels |
| 3. Hard | "Get over yourself. Get some clarity." | Letter, hero sections |
| 4. Line in the sand | "You're not ready. Prove us wrong." | Offers, last call |

The default voice is level 2 to 3. Never start at level 4. Level 4 is for one line, at the very end of a page, offer or carousel. Never end below the level you started, and never soften at the call to action.

## Say / do not say
| Do not | Do |
|---|---|
| "We're passionate about helping you grow" | "We do the work. You do the commitment." |
| "Unlock your potential" | "Stop being fine." |
| "Boost your business" | "Build the business. Content follows." |
| "Learn More" | "Show me what I'm doing wrong" |

## Banned
unlock, leverage, empower, seamless, game-changer, solutions, elevate your, at your fingertips, take your business to the next level, we're passionate about, "Learn More" as a button, boost your business.

"Boosting" is the thing we mock, so it only appears when mocking it.

## Humour
Fun, nonchalant, with bite. The Christmas poem proves the register: rhyme and play on the surface, a hard truth under it. Never jokes at the reader's expense that leave no way out.

## Language
English first. Arabic and mixed use are an open decision (see Open decisions).

# Visual

## Principles
- Dark, navy-based. No pastels. No generic corporate blue and white.
- Nautical metaphors only. No rockets, lightbulbs or handshakes.
- Intensity rises with depth: darker, tighter, bolder as the reader goes down. A page that looks the same from top to bottom is a bug.
- No stock photography. No soft SaaS cards, default checkmarks or feature grids.
- Hard corners. `radius-0` is the default.

## Colour
- **Ground:** `ink-900`. Panels `ink-700`. Depth `ink-500`.
- **Text:** `off-white`. Captions `text-muted`.
- **Accent:** `rust` for labels, progress and calls to action.
- **Highlight:** `gold`, on one word per screen.
- **Warning:** `magenta` and `alarm`, small areas only.

Proportion on any layout: about 70% navy, 20% off-white, 8% rust, 2% gold.

## Type
- **Anton**, uppercase, for headlines and poem lines. Tracking 0.5px, leading 1.1.
- **IBM Plex Sans** for body.
- **IBM Plex Mono** 500, uppercase, 4px tracking, for labels, counters and handles.

Fonts load from Google Fonts. No font files are stored in this book.

## Logo
An orange sail rising out of navy waves, with THE HARD PORT stacked in three lines of bold sans. The sail is the "hard a-port" turn, the waves are the sea we keep naming.

- **On light grounds:** `thp-logo-primary.png` (navy and orange).
- **On dark grounds:** `thp-logo-on-dark.png` (off-white and orange). The navy wordmark disappears on `ink-900`, so never place the primary version on the night theme.
- **Clear space:** at least the height of the letter T on every side.
- **Minimum size:** 120px wide on screen.
- **Never:** recolour the sail, stretch, tilt, add shadows, put it on photos without a navy scrim, or retype the name in another font beside it.
- **Placement:** top left or bottom left. In carousels the footer stays text (THE HARD PORT in mono). The full logo goes on the first and last slide only.
- **The sail alone:** the sail and waves work as a profile picture and favicon. Cut it from a vector file, not from the PNG.

The logo uses a brighter royal navy (`logo-navy`) and orange (`logo-orange`) than the night palette. Both live side by side for now. See Open decisions.

# Illustration

## Style
Low-poly night forest. Flat triangular facets lit from the top left. Snow, pines, a ridge line, a spotlight beam or a glow as the focal light. Vignette at the edges. Dark and calm with one warm light source per scene.

# Offer and funnel

## The website ladder
Traffic arrives cold from a short "you vs you" video. The site converts. Every section must earn the next scroll.

1. **Hook (video).** Sets up "you vs you", ends on a line that resolves on the site.
2. **Identity match.** "Your business isn't sinking, it's not going anywhere." Lands in the first 3 seconds of scroll.
3. **Agitation.** The canonical letter.
4. **Mechanism.** Boosting versus a campaign.
5. **Proof.** Honest anti-testimonial stats. The gap in normal proof becomes the proof.
6. **Offer stack.** A ladder, named for how brave the owner is: Port, Sea, Ocean.
7. **Scarcity.** Two free slots a month. We pick, the applicant does not. Real, never a fake timer.
8. **Objections.** Every answer matches the aggression of its question.
9. **Last call.** The hardest line on the page. One call to action, nothing competing.

## Calls to action escalate
Never the same label twice.
- Top: "Watch the truth" / "See the gap"
- Middle: "Show me what I'm doing wrong"
- Near price: "Prove us wrong"
- Bottom: "Stop being fine" / "Apply for the free slot"

## What we sell
**The Port programme:** 30 weeks from running on the owner to running on a system. Five sprints: Structure, Costs, Profit, Clients, Team. Plus a done-for-you website and online presence, 1:1 partnership calls and a community. The prices in the 21 Oct document ($6,000 split, $4,500 in full, then $325 a month) were written for the earlier 23-week version and are not confirmed for 30 weeks. Testers work with us for little money (see Solution model brief). "A transformation, not a course." It starts by diagnosing Point A and moves to a measurable Point B.

**The website tiers (earlier version):**
| Tier | Price | For |
|---|---|---|
| To the Port | custom | People still scared of the water |
| To the Sea | $632 a month | People who want to move |
| To the Ocean | $1,723 a month | People who are done playing small |

These two price structures conflict. See Open decisions.

Right now we are only working on the Port product, and we are looking for the first testers. Sea and Ocean are parked until the Port is proven. Do not show three tiers on the site until then.

## Frameworks behind it
- **Hormozi value equation.** Raise dream outcome and believability. Cut time delay and effort.
- **Godin.** Smallest viable audience. Remarkable over safe. Marketing creates a tension that only action resolves, so nothing mid-page resolves it early.

## Internal rule
A larger network sits behind The Hard Port. Never name it and never explain it publicly. At most one understated line may imply there is a door beyond this one. Silence about it is part of the gatekeeping tone.
